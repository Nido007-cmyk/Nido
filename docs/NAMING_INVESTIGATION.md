# NIDO — Master Brand Naming Investigation (Research Only)

- **Prepared:** 2026-09-26/27 (research window)
- **Scope:** Future master brand covering company, app, personal agent, agent protocol, agent-to-agent network, hardware, and future products.
- **Codename rule:** **NIDO remains the codename.** Nothing in code, repositories, packages, protocols, cryptographic domain separators, wire identifiers, architecture, or historical documentation is renamed by this investigation.
- **Deliverable:** ~10–15 filtered candidates + 3–5 finalists. **No winner is selected.** Selection is a business decision that must follow attorney-led clearance.

> **This is preliminary knockout research, not legal advice and not attorney-led trademark clearance.**
> Findings are based on public web search, public trademark-database snapshots, GitHub, and registry RDAP observed during the research window.
> They are incomplete by design: App Store / Google Play discoverability checks were not performed, international classes were not exhaustively screened,
> and availability can change at any time. Nothing here is a guarantee of registrability or domain availability.

## 1. Brand requirements (as defined for this investigation)

**Story the name must carry:** the agent belongs to the user; private identity and memory; primarily device-local and works offline;
online capabilities only with user permission; direct agent-to-agent collaboration; the user keeps authority; the model is replaceable.

**Form:** 4–8 letters, 1–3 syllables, pronounceable and spellable in English and Spanish, clean in upper/lowercase,
credible for both consumer and infrastructure use. Must work in: "My ___ handled it", "___ Protocol", "___ Identity", "___ Network", "___ OS".

**Territory explored:** Latin, Greek, Spanish, restrained invented forms.

**Excluded by rule:** `AI + generic`, `Agent + generic`, chatbot/ChatGPT-style names, similarity to major tech or messaging brands,
difficult artificial spelling, childish or cyberpunk names, names tied to current phones/Bluetooth/chat/present-day AI.

## 2. Method and sources

1. Public web search for each candidate (exact match + "software", "AI", "company", "app", "trademark").
2. Public trademark-database snapshots: USPTO via TrademarkElite/TrademarkTok/Furm, EUIPO via trademarkelite/trademarkers, IndiaFilings, Unibrander (LATAM), TTAB filings where surfaced.
3. GitHub organizations/repos/packages for exact-name software projects.
4. Registry RDAP for bare `.com` / `.ai` / `.app` (all 15 original bare names observed as registered; see §5).
5. Phonetic/visual near-match review in EN and ES.

**Gaps (do not treat silence as clearance):** App Store and Google Play discoverability were not checked; `.io`/`.dev` RDAP checks were attempted
but the research environment's network did not return conclusive answers; qualified/modified domains (getX, Xhq, useX, Xapp, Xos) were not
systematically screened; relevant-class trademark screening (classes 9, 35, 38, 42) was partial and must be completed by counsel.

## 4. Candidate entries

### 1. MEMINI — Latin, "I remember"

- **Pronunciation:** EN *meh-MEE-nee*, ES *me-MI-ni* — near-identical, 3 syllables, clean in upper/lowercase.
- **Story:** Private memory is the core of the agent. *Memini* is the first-person "I remember" — the agent's memory belongs to the person, kept on their device, under their authority. It names the product's deepest differentiator (private, persistent, user-owned memory) rather than its plumbing.
- **Strategic fit:** Consumer-warm and infrastructure-credible at once. Memory is also the feature most tied to the 10-year identity/memory/portability principles.
- **EN/ES tests:** Same spelling/pronunciation in both languages; no awkward consonant clusters; "My Memini handled it" reads naturally.
- **Ecosystem test:** "Memini Protocol", "Memini Identity", "Memini Network", "Memini OS" — all clean, no punning.
- **Known collisions:** US mark `88569617` (MEMINI) for hearing aids — different class, unlikely to block software classes but must be checked by counsel; an abandoned camera-product filing. No direct exact software/AI personal-agent conflict found in first pass.
- **Rating:** `MEDIUM COLLISION`.
- **Domains:** Bare `.com`/`.ai`/`.app` observed as registered (per registry RDAP, §5). Qualified forms (`getmemini`, `meminihq`, `memini.app` variants) not systematically screened.
- **Strengths:** Unique in the AI space; story maps to the privacy/memory architecture; pronounceable identically in EN/ES; 6 letters.
- **Weaknesses:** Hearing-aid mark creates clearance work; 3 syllables is at the long end of the form brief; common "memory" root may invite adjacent memory-product confusion (e.g. the crowded *Memora* space — checked and excluded, §5).

### 2. AMPARO — Spanish, "shelter, protection, support"

- **Pronunciation:** EN *ahm-PAH-ro*, ES *am-PA-ro* — 3 syllables, warm, no difficult sounds.
- **Story:** The agent *shelters* your private life. In one ordinary Spanish word: protection, support, refuge — the product's privacy promise as an everyday concept, not a technical claim. Also carries the Latin-American legal resonance of *juicio de amparo* (protection of rights), which is on-brand for a user-authority product but must be handled carefully (see weaknesses).
- **Strategic fit:** Strongest Spanish-language emotional fit in the pool; credible as a consumer brand and dignified as an infrastructure name. Unusual for AI brands, which is a defensibility advantage in a sea of Latin/Greek coinages.
- **EN/ES tests:** EN speakers pronounce it cleanly on first read; ES is native. "My Amparo handled it" works; "Amparo Protocol", "Amparo Identity", "Amparo Network", "Amparo OS" all work.
- **Ecosystem test:** No punning; reads as a proper name, not a descriptor — good for trademark strength *if* it clears.
- **Known collisions:** First-pass results surfaced only unrelated marks (healthcare, finance, apparel, cleaning services) — no software, AI, or communications conflicts found. Relevant-class (9/38/42) screening was partial and must be completed by counsel.
- **Rating:** `LOW-MEDIUM COLLISION` — the cleanest exact-conflict picture in the pool, downgraded only for distinctiveness reasons below.
- **Domains:** Bare `.com`/`.ai`/`.app` observed as registered (§5). Qualified forms not screened.
- **Strengths:** Best meaning-to-product fit in Spanish; no exact AI/software collision found; warm consumer brand with serious infrastructure potential; 6 letters.
- **Weaknesses:** Ordinary Spanish word → weak inherent distinctiveness (harder to register, easier to coexist against); also a common personal name in Spanish/Portuguese → permanent search noise; the *amparo* legal concept in LATAM could create unintended legal-service connotations; "Amparo" may read as a person's name in consumer contexts ("Amparo called" vs "Amparo handled it").

- **Story:** The threshold between your private world and the network. The agent stands *at the umbral*: nothing crosses without your permission. This is the product's architecture (local-first, permissioned online) expressed as a single image — arguably the most honest name in the pool.

- **EN/ES tests:** EN speakers will approximate acceptably; spelling is unambiguous. "My Umbral handled it" works.
- **Ecosystem test:** Clean across protocol/identity/network/OS constructions.
- **Known collisions:** Abandoned US drinkware application (`97294177`, dead); an active GitHub admin-framework project using the name; **near-match `UMBRA` — secure-networking technology** (phonetic proximity in the networking/security space is the key concern); the word appears in some games/dev tools.
- **Rating:** `MEDIUM COLLISION`.
- **Domains:** Bare `.com`/`.ai`/`.app` observed as registered (§5). Qualified forms not screened.

- **Weaknesses:** The `UMBRA` secure-networking near-match is the one real knockout-grade risk and needs counsel's phonetic-similarity opinion; EN pronunciation will drift from Spanish; a GitHub project already occupies the name in developer tooling.

## 8. If a public rebrand ever happens: engineering rules

These rules protect the product's stability and security regardless of which public name is chosen. They are stated now so a future rebrand cannot accidentally become a breaking change:

1. **NIDO stays as the technical identifier.** Do not casually rename wire identifiers, protocol version strings, package coordinates, database migration names, cryptographic domain separators, repository names, or historical tags/commits. Renaming any of these is a migration project, not a find-and-replace.
2. **The public brand is a presentation layer.** Ship the new brand as a user-facing alias (app name, marketing site, store listings, user-visible strings) while internal identifiers remain stable. Prefer a compatibility-preserving alias over a staged rename wherever possible.
3. **Cryptographic domain separators are never retroactively changed.** A domain separator is a security boundary; changing it invalidates existing signatures, pairings, and audit trails. If a new separator is ever required, it must be introduced as a **new versioned value** with dual support during a transition window — never by editing history.
4. **Protocol migration, if ever needed, is version-gated.** Any future identifier change on the wire must be negotiated by protocol version, announced in both old and new forms during transition, and validated by the conformance suite before rollout.
5. **Historical documentation keeps NIDO references.** Add a codename note ("NIDO was the development codename for \<public brand\>") rather than rewriting history. Rewriting history breaks archaeology for security review.
6. **No rebrand work begins before C-1 closes.** Naming changes touch user-visible surfaces and documentation; the verification phase must not be destabilized.
