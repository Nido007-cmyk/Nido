# NIDO Group Tasks — Specification (draft v0.1)

**Status:** design, NOT implemented yet. **No group cryptography
yet**: this document designs multi-party coordination over
existing pairwise channels (authenticated 1:1 sessions). Group
cryptography remains an open question (§12.6).

**Future dependency:** `docs/CAPABILITY_NEGOTIATION.md` (not yet written)
will define the fine-grained semantics of `PROPOSE` / `COUNTER` /
`ACCEPT` / `DECLINE` / `EXPIRE`. This document composes it in a multi-party
context; in case of conflict, the negotiation semantics win and this
document is revised.

**Inviolable principles:**

- **THE MODEL IS NOT NIDO.**
- *"A request can describe what another NIDO wants. It can never define
  what this NIDO is authorized to do."*
- Each Policy Engine decides **locally**; the group does not vote on permissions.
- Minimum disclosure also **between participants**.
- `TRANSPORT ≠ TRUST`. Fail-closed.

---

## 1. Normative case

> "Find when the four of us can meet."

A, B, C and D want a common time slot. **No agent receives the others'
calendars.** Each NIDO computes its availability **locally** (reads its
own calendar, applies its policy) and reveals only what is needed
to converge: compatible intervals or votes on proposed slots.

What the group learns at the end: *"we're meeting Tuesday 18:30–19:30"*.
What the group does **not** learn: why B couldn't do 17:00, which
calendar C uses, or which model D runs.

## 2. Multi-party task model

### 2.1 Identifiers and membership

- `group_task_id`: 128 random bits, generated by the initiator.
  Group-lifecycle idempotency key (like `task_id`
  in `AGENT_PROTOCOL.md` §9).
- `members[]`: closed list of NIDO identities (identity public key,
  hex), in canonical order (lexicographic). **Membership is
  fixed at task creation**: nobody joins mid-way without restarting the
  negotiation (prevents participant injection).
- Each group message is a signed envelope of the base protocol
  (`AGENT_PROTOCOL.md` §4): `sender` = identity + device +
  certificate, `signature` of the sending device. Participant authentication
  **reuses** the identity→devices→sessions model;
  nothing new is invented.
- Admission rule: a group message from an identity outside of
  `members[]` → `INVALID_PARTICIPANT`, silent discard + audit.

### 2.2 The coordinator is NOT authority

Any participant can initiate (and becomes the coordinator).
The coordinator:

- **Does:** proposes slots, aggregates responses, recalculates on
  dropouts, notifies the result.
- **Is not:** does not authorize anything on behalf of others, does not grant
  permissions, does not execute others' tools, does not reveal one participant's
  data to another beyond what is aggregated/necessary.
- **What it learns is limited to what was revealed** (§3): it sees exactly what
  each participant chose to reveal per its policy, not one bit more.
- **Deterministic succession:** if the coordinator leaves or stops
  responding (timeout), the next member in the canonical order of
  `members[]` can take over coordination by announcing
  a signed `GROUP_COORDINATOR_TAKEOVER`. No elections, no coordinator
  voting: the canonical order decides.

### 2.3 Partial responses

A participant can respond partially without breaking the task:

- Respond to only a subset of proposed slots.
- Respond `SUPPORTED_WITH_CONSTRAINTS` (e.g. "only 30 min, not 60").
- Ask for more time (`GROUP_DEFER` with a new `eta_ms`).

The task advances with what was received; pending items are governed by timeouts (§6).
A partial response is **never** interpreted as acceptance of the rest.

## 3. Disclosure modes: vote before collecting

Two modes, with different privacy footprints. **Vote mode is the
recommended default.**

### 3.1 Vote mode (default)

1. The coordinator proposes candidate slots
   (`GROUP_PROPOSE`: list of `{start, end}`).
2. Each participant responds per slot: `SUPPORTED` / `UNSUPPORTED` /
   `SUPPORTED_WITH_CONSTRAINTS` — without saying why.
3. The coordinator aggregates and announces the intersection.

**Disclosure per participant:** only their vote on proposed slots.
Nobody reveals their calendar, not even their free intervals.

### 3.2 Collect mode (restricted)

1. The coordinator requests availability in a window
   (`GROUP_AVAILABILITY_REQUEST{window_start, window_end}`).
2. Each participant responds with their free intervals
   (capability `availability.query/v1`, minimum disclosure as in
   `CAPABILITY_MODEL.md` §2).
3. The coordinator computes the intersection.

**Disclosure per participant:** their free intervals in the window —
strictly more than vote mode. Requires explicit policy
(`ALLOW_UNDER_CONDITIONS` or higher); the default is to deny it and suggest
vote mode.

### 3.3 Minimum aggregation rule

The coordinator does **not forward individual responses** to other
participants. It shares only:

- the aggregated result (intersection / chosen slot), or
- anonymized counts if the consensus rule requires them
  ("3 of 4 support Tuesday 18:30"), never *who* voted what, unless the
  task explicitly requires it and each one's policy allows it.

## 4. Channels: pairwise today, group tomorrow

### 4.1 Pairwise (current design — no group crypto)

Each coordinator↔participant pair uses its existing **1:1 session**
(authenticated handshake, AEAD). The coordinator acts as an aggregator of
signed messages.

- **What the coordinator learns:** exactly what each participant revealed
  (§3). It cannot read more: the envelopes are encrypted per session
  and signed by the sending device.
- **What each participant sees:** their own messages + the coordinator's
  aggregates. They don't see others' messages (the coordinator doesn't forward them).
- **What a transport observer sees:** ciphertext (see
  `TRANSPORT_ARCHITECTURE.md` §3).

### 4.2 Group channel (future — DO NOT implement)

A channel with a shared key where everyone sees all messages would reduce
the coordinator's power as an intermediary, but requires group key agreement,
multi-party forward secrecy, and dynamic membership management.
See open question §12.6. **Design decision:** the group protocol
must work identically over pairwise; if a group channel ever exists,
it's one more logical transport, not a semantic change.

### 4.3 Malicious coordinator: hard limits

A Byzantine coordinator **can, at most**:

- drop, delay or duplicate messages (mitigated by timeouts §6,
  idempotency via `message_id`/`group_task_id`, and seen-sets —
  `AGENT_PROTOCOL.md` §9);
- lie in the aggregation (e.g. announce a false intersection).

**It cannot:**

- read beyond what was revealed (per-session encryption + minimum disclosure);
- forge a participant's response (sending-device signature);
- grant itself authority over others (each Policy Engine decides locally);
- make a participant execute something unauthorized (final acceptance
  is local, §7).

**False-aggregation detection:** each participant locally verifies
that the announced final slot is compatible with what they
revealed. If it isn't → `COORDINATOR_FAULT`, abort and audit. The
verification does not require trusting the coordinator.

## 5. Composition with negotiation

(Depends on `CAPABILITY_NEGOTIATION.md` for the fine semantics; here the
multi-party assembly.)

- `GROUP_PROPOSE`: the coordinator proposes to **everyone** (one or more
  slots). Equivalent to a broadcast `PROPOSE`.
- `GROUP_COUNTER`: a participant counter-proposes — **addressed to the
  coordinator**, which decides whether to incorporate it into a new
  `GROUP_PROPOSE` round. Counter-proposals are not negotiated peer-to-peer
  between participants (avoids combinatorial explosion and side leaks).
- `GROUP_ACCEPT` / `GROUP_DECLINE`: per participant and per proposal.
  `ACCEPT` means "this slot works for me **if** the group picks it"; the
  final action with side effects (creating the event) still requires the
  local Policy Engine decision of each one.
- `GROUP_EXPIRE`: expiration of a proposal or of the whole task.

**Consensus rule** (announced by the coordinator in `GROUP_INVITE`,
immutable afterwards):

- `unanimity` — all slots accepted by everyone (default for
  meetings);
- `quorum(n)` — at least `n` acceptances (useful if someone leaves, §8);
- `coordinator_picks` — the coordinator picks among the accepted ones with
  an announced deterministic criterion (e.g. "the earliest one").

**Selection:** with several accepted slots, the announced criterion applies.
The criterion is public and deterministic so any participant can verify the result.

---

## 6. Timeouts and quorum

- **Per-participant timeout** (`response_timeout_ms`, announced in the
  invite; suggested default: 24 h for human tasks): no response →
  the participant is marked `UNRESPONSIVE`. Neither acceptance nor
  rejection is assumed: depending on the consensus rule, either wait,
  or recalculate without them.
- **Global timeout** (`expires_at` of the group): expiration → `GROUP_EXPIRE`
  to everyone still reachable + audit event.
- **Minimum quorum** (`min_participants`): if active members fall below
  → `QUORUM_LOST`, the task fails explicitly (fail-closed).
  A four-person meeting never "completes" with two unless each one's policy
  accepts it again.

---

## 7. Final acceptance is local

Group consensus produces a **final proposal**, not an order. Each
NIDO, on receiving `GROUP_RESULT{chosen_slot}`:

1. Verifies the slot is compatible with what was revealed (anti
   `COORDINATOR_FAULT`).
2. Runs the side-effect action through **its** Policy Engine
   (`calendar.event.create/v1` → typically `ASK_USER` /
   `human_approval: always`).
3. Only then creates the event locally.

An `ACCEPT` in negotiation **never** equals authorization of the
final action. Autonomy ≠ authority.

---

## 8. A participant dropping out mid-process

1. The participant sends a signed `GROUP_WITHDRAW{group_task_id, reason?}`
   (or simply stops responding until the timeout → `UNRESPONSIVE`).
2. The coordinator notifies the rest: `GROUP_PARTICIPANT_UPDATE` (who
   left, without sensitive reason; `reason` is an enum, never free text with
   private data).
3. **Recalculation:** the consensus rule is re-evaluated with the remaining
   members. With `quorum(n)` the task may continue; with `unanimity`
   and one fewer member, either re-propose or fail with `QUORUM_LOST`.
4. **What was already revealed cannot be "un-revealed":** the intervals or votes
   the participant shared before leaving **remain known** to
   the coordinator. This is documented as an honest design limit: leaving
   stops *future* disclosure, it doesn't erase the past. That's why vote mode
   (§3.1) is the default: it minimizes what there is to regret.
5. If the one leaving is the coordinator → deterministic succession (§2.2) or
   `COORDINATOR_LOST` if nobody takes over before the timeout.

## 9. State machine (group task)

```
INVITED ──(todos aceptan invite / quorum invita)──▶ COLLECTING
   │  (rechazo / timeout global)
   ▼
COLLECTING ──(respuestas suficientes)──▶ NEGOTIATING
   │  ◀──(GROUP_COUNTER incorporada: nueva ronda)──┘
   │  (PARTICIPANT_WITHDREW → recalcular; si quorum insuficiente)
   ▼
NEGOTIATING ──(regla de consenso satisfecha)──▶ CONSENSUS_REACHED
   │
   ▼
CONSENSUS_REACHED ──(GROUP_RESULT a cada miembro)──▶ FINALIZING_LOCAL
   │   (cada Policy Engine decide: crear / pedir confirmación / denegar)
   ▼
COMPLETED  (todos los activos finalizaron localmente)
   │
   └──▶ FAILED (QUORUM_LOST | CONSENSUS_TIMEOUT | COORDINATOR_FAULT | EXPIRED)
   └──▶ CANCELLED (GROUP_CANCEL del coordinador o de cualquier miembro
                   para su propia participación)
```

Notes:

- `FINALIZING_LOCAL` is not a shared state: each NIDO walks through it in
  private. The group only sees `GROUP_DONE` / `GROUP_DECLINED_FINAL` per
  participant, without sensitive reasons.
- A local `DENY` in the final phase does **not invalidate** the consensus for the
  others: each one decides their own action. The group reports partial
  completion explicitly.

### 9.1 Group-specific error codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `PARTICIPANT_WITHDREW` | a member left; recalculation or `QUORUM_LOST` attached |
| `QUORUM_LOST` | active members < `min_participants`; fail-closed |
| `CONSENSUS_TIMEOUT` | the consensus rule wasn't reached before `expires_at` |
| `INVALID_PARTICIPANT` | message from an identity outside `members[]` |
| `COORDINATOR_FAULT` | aggregation inconsistent with what was revealed (detected locally) |
| `COORDINATOR_LOST` | the coordinator is not responding and nobody took over |
| `DUPLICATE_VOTE` | same participant votes twice on the same slot (the first counts) |
| `STALE_PROPOSAL` | message about an already-superseded proposal round |

**One vote per identity (round 3, C-9):** the vote is keyed by
**identity**, not by device: a participant who leaves and rejoins
(same or another device) neither votes twice nor resets their
disclosure budgets. `DUPLICATE_VOTE` also covers re-voting after
rejoining.

## 10. Audit (without sensitive content)

Per group task, each NIDO logs locally:

```
{ts, event: group.invite_sent|group.invite_received|group.vote_cast|
        group.withdraw|group.result|group.failed,
 group_task_id, members_count, coordinator, consensus_rule,
 my_disclosure: <categorías reveladas>, policy_decision,
 transport, device_id}
```

*Which categories* were revealed is audited ("votes on 3 slots"),
never the content ("I voted no on Tuesday because…"). A participant
can later answer "why did you do that?" with this chain
(see `AUTONOMY_MODEL.md` for explainability).

---

## 11. Composition with the rest of the architecture

- **Policy Engine:** each incoming group message passes the
  `CAPABILITY_MODEL.md` §4 pipeline. `GROUP_AVAILABILITY_REQUEST` is evaluated
  as `availability.query/v1` against the receiver's local policy.
- **Privacy budget:** voting/collecting rounds consume disclosure
  budget (see `AUTONOMY_MODEL.md`); a coordinator asking for 100
  rounds to reconstruct calendars hits rate limits and policy denial.
- **Transport:** group messages are ordinary envelopes; they travel over
  any transport (`TRANSPORT_ARCHITECTURE.md`). The group neither knows nor
  cares whether B is on Bluetooth and C on LAN.
- **Model:** the local model may *suggest* candidate slots or
  draft the summary for the user; it **never** decides votes, accepts
  proposals, or authorizes disclosure. Reasoning ≠ Authority.

## 12. Open questions

1. Should `members[]` support late addition with re-consent from
   everyone (useful: "let's invite E too"), or is the current rigidity
   the correct protection?
2. In collect mode, should the coordinator cryptographically prove
   that the announced intersection is correct without revealing inputs
   (private set intersection)? Is the complexity worth it for the use
   case?
3. How is a group negotiation presented to the user without fatigue
   ("3 groups are waiting for you")? A proposal inbox with visible expiration?
4. Does `GROUP_DEFER` need limits (a participant can't postpone
   indefinitely a quorum task)?
5. Does a "silent mode" make sense where votes are aggregated without the
   coordinator seeing individual votes (blind aggregation)? Or is it
   over-engineering compared to simple vote mode?
6. **What group cryptography to evaluate in the future (without implementing
   it now)?** Candidates to study: **MLS (RFC 9420)** — IETF standard
   for group messaging with forward secrecy and post-compromise security,
   but designed for large groups and with some operational complexity;
   **Signal-style sender keys** — simpler for small stable groups, but worse PCS
   on compromise; **staying pairwise** —
   maximum simplicity and the current design already works this way, at the cost of
   trusting the coordinator as aggregator. Evaluation criteria
   when the time comes: real forward secrecy and PCS, CPU/battery cost on
   mobile, auditable implementation complexity, dynamic membership management,
   and that it **never** grants the group authority over the local Policy
   Engine. No option is adopted for maximizing theoretical anonymity;
   NIDO prioritizes practical, verifiable privacy.
