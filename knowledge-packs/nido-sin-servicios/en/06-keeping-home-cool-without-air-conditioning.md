The NIDO Essentials extreme heat and cold guide gives the general advice for a heatwave. This guide goes into the detail of the house itself: how to measure whether it is getting too warm, how to ventilate at night, and when a fan stops protecting you.

## Measure the temperature in your home
- Check the room temperature **between 08:00 and 10:00, at 13:00 and at night after 22:00**.
- Ideally, keep it **below 32°C (90°F) during the day and below 24°C (75°F) at night**. This matters most for infants, people over 60 and people with chronic health conditions.
- If your home will not come down below those figures, doing "everything below" is not optional — and neither is spending hours somewhere cool (see the end).

## By day: keep the heat out
- **Close the windows** when it is hotter outside than inside, and cover sun-facing windows with blinds, shutters or curtains to block direct sunlight.
- Hang shades, awnings or louvers on windows that receive morning or afternoon sun: shading from outside stops the heat before it gets in.
- Turn off as many electrical devices and lights as you can: every device left on adds heat inside the home.
- Cook as little as possible during the hot hours, and move to the coolest room in the home, especially at night.

## At night: let the heat out
- **Use the night air to cool down your home**: open windows after dark, when the outdoor temperature is lower than indoors, and leave them open through the night and early morning if it is safe to do so.
- Hang wet towels to cool down the room air.
- Sleep with light bed linen and light, loose-fitting clothing, and no cushions: they accumulate heat.

## The fan: how far it goes
- A fan may provide relief, but **when the temperature is above 35°C (95°F), it may not prevent heat-related illness**, even if the air feels pleasant on your face.
- Use electric fans **only when temperatures are below 40°C (104°F)**: above 40°C, fans will heat the body (WHO).
- If there is no power or no fan, cool the body directly: cool showers or baths, wetting your skin with a damp cloth, a spray bottle or wet clothing, and foot baths in cool water (WHO and CDC).

## Your body, meanwhile
- Drink water regularly: **1 cup of water per hour and at least 2–3 litres per day**. Avoid alcohol and too much caffeine.
- Regularly check in with vulnerable people around you: people over 65, and those with heart, lung or kidney conditions, a disability, or who live alone.
- Never leave children or animals in a parked vehicle, not even for a moment.

## When the house is no longer enough
If it is not possible to keep your home cool, spend **2–3 hours of the day in a cool place**: an air-conditioned public building, a library, a shopping mall or a cooling center opened by the authorities. It may even be cooler outdoors, in the shade, than inside.

Heat stroke is an emergency that can kill: if someone feels faint, dizzy or nauseous, stops sweating, shows signs of confusion or loses consciousness, get medical help immediately and cool them with cold, wet cloths while you wait (see the NIDO Essentials heat stroke guide).
