With no gas or electricity you can still eat hot food, but there is one line that is never crossed: **everything that burns fuel to cook belongs outside the house**. Carbon monoxide cannot be seen or smelled, it builds up in enclosed spaces and can kill silently.

## The simplest option: don't cook
- Commercially canned food may be eaten right out of the can, without warming.
- Keep food on hand that needs no cooking and no water: ready-to-eat canned foods, bars, nuts, dried fruit, peanut butter, crackers.

## Indoors: warming only, with a small, watched flame
According to Ready.gov, alternative sources for warming food indoors in an emergency are:
- **Candle warmers**, **chafing dishes** and **fondue pots**: they warm food that is **already cooked**, but they do not really cook. They cannot be used for raw meat, fish, poultry or eggs: cooking thoroughly means the food reaches about **70°C (158°F)** all the way through (WHO), and a candle does not get there.
- **A fireplace that is up to code**: only if the fireplace and chimney are in good condition and vented to the outside. Never burn charcoal indoors, not even in a fireplace, and do not burn paper in the fireplace.

To heat food in its own can: remove the label, thoroughly wash and disinfect the can (1 part bleach to 10 parts water) and **open the can before heating it**.

Any flame lit indoors is watched the whole time and put out when you finish.

## Outdoors: that is where real fire belongs
- **Charcoal or gas grills** and **camp stoves**: **outdoors only**, away from doors and windows. Never inside a home, garage, basement, tent or camper — not even with the windows open.
- Generators follow the same rule: always outdoors, **at least 20 feet (6 meters)** from any window, door or vent.
- Before lighting anything after a disaster, check the cooking area for damage. If you smell gas, do not light anything.

## Cook only what that meal needs
- Without a refrigerator, cooked food cannot sit at room temperature for more than **2 hours**.
- Prepare just enough for that meal, serve it straight away and do not keep leftovers out of the cold.
- Keep cooking and eating utensils clean, keep food in covered containers, and wash your hands before cooking.

## What is never done
- Never use a camp stove, a grill or burning charcoal indoors "just for a moment": it is the classic cause of carbon monoxide poisoning after power outages.
- Never use a gas stove or oven to heat your home.
- If anyone develops a headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea or confusion while any fuel-burning device is in use, get everyone outside into fresh air at once and get help: those are the signs of carbon monoxide poisoning.
