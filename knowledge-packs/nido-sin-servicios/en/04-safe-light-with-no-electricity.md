When the power goes out, the temptation is to light candles. A candle is an open flame in a dark house, with people moving around and curious children: candles are a known cause of home fires during power outages. Safe light is light that does not burn.

## The rule: flashlights, not candles
- Use **battery-powered flashlights and lanterns**, rather than candles, gas lanterns or torches, to minimize the risk of fire (CDC).
- The Red Cross says the same: avoid candles because of the fire risk and use battery-powered lights and flashlights instead.
- Have **a flashlight for every household member** (Ready.gov), kept where everyone knows to find it and can reach it in the dark: by the bed and near the door.
- Store spare batteries with the flashlights and check them from time to time, just as you check the first aid kit.
- A headlamp leaves your hands free for cooking, stairs or looking after a child.
- Save your phone: its light drains the battery you may need to call for help or hear warnings.

## If a candle is truly the only option
Only when no battery light is available at all, and under these conditions, without exception:
- In a sturdy holder, on a stable surface that cannot catch fire.
- **Away from anything that can catch fire**: curtains, paper, furniture, bedding.
- **Never leave a lit candle unattended**: if you leave the room or go to sleep, put it out. Always stay near a lit candle.
- Out of reach of children and pets, and away from drafts that could knock it over or blow the flame onto something.
- Keep a fire extinguisher handy, and make sure your family knows how to use it.

## What to avoid
- Fuel-burning lamps and lanterns (gas, kerosene) indoors: they add both fire risk and carbon monoxide risk.
- Lighting your way with a camp stove, a grill or any flame meant for cooking or heating: that flame belongs outdoors, or in a properly vented fireplace — never as a "lamp".
- Leaving candles burning as a night light: that is what a small flashlight by the bed is for.

## When the power comes back
Put the flashlights back in their place, replace used batteries and note what was missing: next time, the house should be able to go dark without improvising flames.
