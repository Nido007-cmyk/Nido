If the heating fails while it is cold outside, the goal is not to heat the whole house: it is to conserve the heat you have and keep people warm. The NIDO Essentials shelter and body heat guide covers cold outdoors; this one is for indoors.

**The danger, in two lines:** never heat your home with a gas oven or stove, or with charcoal burners, grills or camp stoves indoors: they produce carbon monoxide, which cannot be seen or smelled and kills (see the NIDO Essentials poisoning and carbon monoxide guide). If you feel sick, dizzy or weak, get to fresh air right away — do not delay.

## One room for everyone
1. Choose the smallest, most interior room and gather the whole household there, pets included.
2. Close off the rooms you are not using.
3. Avoid opening doors or windows unnecessarily: every opening lets heat escape.

## Seal the gaps
- Stuff towels or rags in the cracks under doors.
- At night, close draperies or cover windows with blankets: glass is where much of the heat escapes.
- Check the room temperature often if there are babies or older adults: they lose body heat more easily.

## People: layers and blankets
- Dress in **several layers of loose-fitting clothing**, indoors too: a hat, thick socks and mittens if needed, and the same for sleeping.
- Extra blankets, sleeping bags and warm winter coats are your safest "heat source" (CDC).
- Stay dry: wet clothing chills the body quickly. If you sweat, remove a layer.
- Eat well-balanced meals and drink warm, sweet beverages or broth. **Avoid alcohol and caffeine**: they cause your body to lose heat faster.
- Infants under 1 year old should never sleep in a cold room: they lose body heat more easily than adults. Dress them in footed pajamas or wearable blankets (sleep sacks), with no pillows or soft bedding around them, and if the home cannot be kept warm, make arrangements to stay somewhere else.

## Heat sources with fire: only the real ones
- **Fireplace or wood stove**: only if they are properly vented to the outside and do not leak into the indoor air. Do not burn paper in a fireplace, and use only the fuel the heater was designed for.
- **Portable electric space heater** (if there is power but no heating, or with a generator): keep it **at least 3 feet (1 meter)** away from drapes, furniture and bedding, never cover it, never place it on furniture or near water, and never leave children unattended near it. Turn it off when you leave the room or go to sleep.
- Keep a battery-operated carbon monoxide detector in the home and make sure it works.

## When it is time to leave
If the home cannot be kept warm, do not just wait and hope: go to the home of family or friends, a public library, or a warming center opened by the authorities (Red Cross).

Watch for hypothermia indoors too: constant shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands or slurred speech. If a person's temperature falls below **95°F (35°C)**, it is a medical emergency. The NIDO Essentials hypothermia guide explains what to do.
