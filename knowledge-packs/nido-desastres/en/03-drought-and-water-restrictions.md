A drought is a long period with much less rainfall than normal: streams and reservoirs fall, and the water level in wells drops. Unlike other disasters, it arrives slowly and lasts for months. Preparing is mostly one thing: conserve water, and make it part of your daily life before restrictions arrive.

## Before a drought: hunt down leaks and waste
1. **Repair dripping faucets** by replacing washers. One drop per second wastes 2,700 gallons of water a year.
2. Check all plumbing for leaks and have any leaks repaired.
3. Never pour water down the drain when there may be another use for it: water from rinsing vegetables, or captured while waiting for it to get hot, can water your indoor plants or garden.
4. Retrofit all household faucets by installing aerators with flow restrictors, and replace your showerhead with an ultra-low-flow version.
5. Consider purchasing a low-volume toilet that uses less than half the water of older models.
6. Insulate your water pipes to reduce heat loss and prevent them from breaking.
7. In the kitchen, instead of using the garbage disposal, throw food in the garbage or start a compost pile: disposals require a lot of water to operate properly.
8. Check your well pump periodically: if the automatic pump turns on and off while water is not being used, you have a leak.

## During a drought: observe the restrictions
Always observe state and local restrictions on water use during a drought. If restricted, for example, do not water your lawn, wash your car, or make other non-essential uses, to help ensure there is enough water for essential uses. Contact your local water provider for current information and suggestions.

### Indoors
- Avoid flushing the toilet unnecessarily. Dispose of tissues, insects and other similar waste in the trash rather than the toilet.
- Take short showers instead of baths. Turn on the water only to get wet and lather, and then again to rinse off.
- Avoid letting the water run while brushing your teeth, washing your face or shaving.
- Operate clothes washers and automatic dishwashers only when they are fully loaded. Use the "light wash" feature of the dishwasher to use less water.
- Clean vegetables in a pan filled with water rather than running water from the tap.
- Avoid using running water to thaw meat or other frozen foods: defrost food overnight in the refrigerator.

### Outdoors
- If your lawn does require watering, do so early in the morning or later in the evening, when temperatures are cooler, and water in several short sessions rather than one long one so the lawn can better absorb moisture and avoid runoff.
- Avoid leaving sprinklers or hoses unattended: a garden hose can pour out 600 gallons or more in only a few hours.
- Use a broom instead of a hose to clean leaves and other debris from your driveway or sidewalk.
- In extreme drought, allow lawns to die in favour of preserving trees and large shrubs.
- Use a commercial car wash that recycles water; if you wash your own car, use a shut-off nozzle on your hose.

## After
- Keep the saving habits in wet years too: the next drought goes better with an already efficient home.
- Plant native and drought-tolerant grasses, ground covers, shrubs and trees, grouped together based on similar water needs: once established, they won't need as much watering.
