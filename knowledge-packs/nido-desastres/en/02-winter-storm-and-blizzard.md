A winter storm can last a few hours or several days, bringing extreme cold, freezing rain, snow, ice and high winds. A blizzard adds wind-driven snow that can drop visibility to near zero. At home, the biggest danger is being cut off: no heat, no power and no communications for days at a time. This guide is about that isolation; for what cold does to the body, see the hypothermia and the heat and extreme cold guides in the Essentials pack.

## Know your winter weather terms
- **Winter Storm Warning**: hazardous winter weather (heavy snow, heavy freezing rain or heavy sleet) is imminent or occurring; usually issued 12 to 24 hours before the event is expected to begin.
- **Winter Storm Watch**: alerts the public to the possibility of a blizzard, heavy snow, heavy freezing rain or heavy sleet; usually issued 12 to 48 hours before the beginning of a winter storm.
- **Winter Weather Advisory**: issued for accumulations of snow, freezing rain, freezing drizzle and sleet that will cause significant inconveniences and, if caution is not exercised, could lead to life-threatening situations.

## Before: prepare the home to be cut off
1. Prepare your home to keep out the cold with insulation, caulking and weather stripping.
2. Learn how to keep pipes from freezing: insulate the pipes you can and, in severe cold, let the cold water drip from a faucet served by exposed pipes; running water, even at a trickle, helps prevent pipes from freezing (Red Cross).
3. Gather supplies in case you need to stay home for several days without power: water, food that needs no cooking, each person's medication, food and water for your pets, and extra batteries for radios and flashlights.
4. Install and test smoke alarms and carbon monoxide detectors with battery backups.
5. Keep a full tank of gas and create an emergency supply kit for your car: jumper cables, sand, a flashlight, warm clothes, blankets, bottled water and non-perishable snacks.

## During: stay inside
1. **Stay off roads if at all possible.** If you are trapped in your car, then stay inside: it is your shelter.
2. Limit your time outside. If you need to go outside, wear layers of warm clothing and watch for signs of frostbite and hypothermia.
3. Reduce the risk of a heart attack by avoiding overexertion when shovelling snow and walking in the snow: rest, and push the snow instead of lifting it when you can.
4. **Carbon monoxide:** only use generators and grills outdoors and away from windows, and never heat your home with a gas stovetop or oven.
5. If the power goes out, follow the power outage guide in the Essentials pack: flashlights instead of candles, keep the fridge closed, and check on older or sick neighbours.

## After
- Walk and drive carefully: icy sidewalks and roads cause falls and crashes.
- If you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, suspect a frozen pipe. Keep the faucet open and apply heat to the frozen section with an electric heating pad, a hair dryer or towels soaked in hot water — never an open flame — until full water pressure is restored; if you cannot locate the frozen area, cannot reach it or cannot thaw it, call a licensed plumber. Check all other faucets: if one pipe froze, others may have frozen too (Red Cross).
- Check on others: older adults, children, sick individuals and pets are at greater risk from the cold.
