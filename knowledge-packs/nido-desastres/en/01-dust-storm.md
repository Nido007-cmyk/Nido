A dust storm is a wall of dust and debris pushed by the wind. It can be miles long and thousands of feet high, it arrives suddenly, and it can drop visibility to near zero in seconds. Blinding, choking dust causes chain collisions on roads. Dust storms usually last only a few minutes, but the actions you take during those minutes matter a lot.

## Before
- In dry and desert areas, check the forecast: strong thunderstorm winds can start a dust storm. Your National Weather Service forecast office issues a Dust Storm Warning if one is happening in your area.
- If you see a wall of dust approaching, do not keep going toward it: find shelter or change your route before it reaches you.

## During: at home or in a building
1. Go inside a sturdy building and stay inside until it passes.
2. Close all windows and turn off all air conditioning units so less dust gets in.
3. Stay away from windows: strong wind can throw objects and break glass.

## During: driving (the most dangerous place)
1. **Don't enter the dust storm area if you can avoid it.** If dense dust is already blowing across the roadway, pull off as soon as possible; do not wait until poor visibility makes it hard to do safely.
2. Pull your vehicle off the pavement as far as possible, completely off the travelled roadway. Never stop on the travelled portion of the roadway.
3. Stop, set the emergency brake and **turn off all lights**, including emergency flashers. Take your foot off the brake pedal so the tail lights are not illuminated: in low visibility, drivers tend to follow the lights they see and may hit your parked car thinking it is moving.
4. Stay in the vehicle with your seat belt buckled and wait for the dust to clear.
5. If you can't pull off the roadway, proceed at a speed suitable for visibility, turn on lights and sound the horn occasionally. Use the painted centre line to help guide you while you look for a safe place to pull off.

## After
- Wait until visibility is back before driving again; there may be crashed vehicles or debris on the road.
- Watch for downed power lines and objects blown around by the wind.
