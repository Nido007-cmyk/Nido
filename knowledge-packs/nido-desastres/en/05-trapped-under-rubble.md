The earthquake guide in the Essentials pack sums this case up in one line; this guide gives it in full: what to do, minute by minute, if an earthquake or another disaster leaves you trapped under rubble or inside a damaged building while rescue is on its way.

## First: protect yourself and stay calm
1. **Stay calm.** Panic burns the strength and the air you need to wait for rescue (CENAPRED).
2. Cover your mouth with your shirt, a handkerchief or any piece of clothing: the dust in rubble damages your lungs.
3. Do not move about or kick up dust, and move as little as possible so you don't disturb what is around you.
4. **Do not light a match**, a lighter or a candle: there may be leaking gas or other fuel among the debris.
5. Check your own body without moving much: if you are bleeding, press on the wound with a clean cloth if you can reach it.

## Make yourself found: signal without shouting
1. **Tap on a pipe or wall** with something solid, in regular bursts: rescuers can hear the tapping through the structure. CENAPRED puts it this way: if you are trapped, stay calm and try to communicate with the outside by banging on an object.
2. Use a whistle if one is available (the one in your go-bag) instead of your voice.
3. If you have your phone with you, **send a text** saying where you are and how you are: texts may be more reliable than phone calls, they save battery power, and they can get through when coverage comes and goes.
4. **Shout only as a last resort:** shouting can cause you to inhale dangerous amounts of dust, and it tires you out fast.
5. Between bursts of tapping, listen: when you hear voices or rescue work nearby, tap again, harder.

## While you wait
- Expect aftershocks if it was an earthquake: cover your head with whatever you can reach, and do not push debris that may be holding weight above you.
- Do not try to lift heavy debris or force a way out blindly: you could bring down another collapse.
- If other people are trapped near you, talk quietly to calm each other and take turns tapping: that way you don't all spend your strength at once.
- Save your phone battery: use it only for short texts, not for long calls or a continuous flashlight.

## If you are not buried, but the building is damaged
1. If you can get out without passing under damaged areas, go outside and move away from the building quickly. Do not enter damaged buildings, and don't stay next to them.
2. Do not use the elevator.
3. If the building creaks, there is fire, or you smell gas and you cannot get out, go back to trapped mode: cover your mouth, move a few metres to safer ground if you can do it safely, and signal by tapping or texting.

## For those searching from outside
- Do not try to remove heavy debris by yourself: rescuing buried people is work for specialised teams, and moving the wrong piece can collapse the pocket where someone is breathing. Tell emergency services the exact place where tapping or voices are heard, and keep periodic silence so listening is possible.
