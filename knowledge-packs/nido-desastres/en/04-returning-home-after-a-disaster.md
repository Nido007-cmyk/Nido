Returning to a home damaged by a flood, a hurricane or an earthquake can be dangerous: live wires, gas leaks, contaminated water and structural damage hide in plain sight. This guide is the full check, step by step, before you enter, at the door and inside.

## Before returning
1. **Return only when authorities say it is safe to do so.** Keep listening to the radio or local news for extended rainfall, aftershocks or further flooding.
2. Take with you: a flashlight and extra batteries, protective clothing with sturdy waterproof boots and work gloves, and a camera or your phone to take pictures of damage for insurance purposes.
3. Leave children and pets with a relative or friend while you conduct your first inspection: the site may be unsafe for children, and hazardous sites and floodwater are dangerous for pets too.
4. Carry some cash: ATMs may not work and stores may not be able to accept credit or debit cards.

## First: look at the home from outside
1. Check the outside of your home before you enter. Look for loose power lines, broken or damaged gas lines, foundation cracks, missing support beams or other damage. Damage on the outside can indicate a serious problem inside.
2. If power lines are down outside your home, do not step in puddles or standing water. Report them immediately to the power company.
3. Do not cut or walk past coloured tape that was placed over doors or windows to mark damaged areas unless you have been told that it is safe to do so. If a building inspector has placed a colour-coded sign on the home, do not enter it until you get more information, advice and instructions from your local authorities.
4. If you have any doubts about safety, ask a building inspector or contractor to check the structure before you enter.
5. Beware of snakes, insects and other animals that may be in or around your home. As you inspect, tap loudly and often on the floor with a stick to give notice that you are there.

## Do not enter if…
- You smell gas or hear a hissing noise (see below: get out and call for help).
- Floodwaters remain around the building.
- Your home was damaged by fire and the authorities have not declared it safe.
- You can see serious damage outside: cracked foundation, fallen supports, a shifted roof or walls.
- The ceiling or floor is sagging: a sagging ceiling got wet, which makes it heavy and dangerous and it could collapse; a sagging floor could collapse under your weight, so don't walk there.

## At the door and going in
1. **Sniff for gas.** If you detect natural or propane gas, or hear a hissing noise, leave the property immediately and get far away from it. Call the fire department after you reach safety.
2. If the door is jammed, don't force it open: it may be providing support to the rest of your home. Find another way to get inside.
3. If power is out, use a flashlight. Do not use any open flame, including candles, to inspect for damage or serve as alternate lighting.
4. Take pictures of home damage, both of the building and its contents, for insurance purposes. Make temporary repairs such as covering holes and bracing walls, and save all receipts.
5. If the weather is dry, open windows and doors to ventilate and dry your home.

## Check the utilities: electricity, gas, water and sewage
- **Electricity:** if there is damage to the electrical system, turn off the system at the main circuit breaker or fuse box. If any gas or electrical appliances were flooded, don't use them until they have been checked for safety.
- **Gas:** utilities should be turned back on by a professional. If you have a propane tank system, turn off all valves and contact a propane supplier to check the system out before you use it again.
- **Water:** do not drink tap water until the authorities say it is safe. Check for water leaks: if water leaks are suspected, shut off the water at the main water valve.
- **Sewage:** check the sewage lines are intact before flushing toilets. Service damaged septic tanks, cesspools, pits and leaching systems as soon as possible.
- **Flooded basement:** pump it out gradually, about one-third of the water per day, to avoid structural damage: if the water is pumped out completely in a short period of time, pressure from water-saturated soil on the outside could cause basement walls to collapse.

## Food and cleaning up
- Throw out all food, beverages and medicine exposed to flood waters and mud, including canned goods and containers with food or liquid that have been sealed shut. When in doubt, throw it out.
- Throw out items that absorb water and cannot be cleaned or disinfected: mattresses, carpeting, cosmetics, stuffed animals and baby toys.
- Wear protective clothing, including rubber gloves and rubber boots, and be careful when moving furnishings or debris, because they may be waterlogged and heavier than they look.

## Important
