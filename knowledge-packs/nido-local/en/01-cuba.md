In Cuba, the emergency numbers are 104 for an ambulance, 105 for the fire department and 106 for the police (Policía Nacional Revolucionaria). According to the Spanish Consulate in Havana, from a mobile phone you dial a 7 first.

## Who warns about a hurricane
Cuba's National Civil Defense Staff declares the phases for a tropical cyclone, province by province. They are announced on radio and television: without internet, a battery-powered radio is the best way to keep up.

## The four phases
1. **Information phase:** the first one. The cyclone is still far away but could affect the island. Follow the bulletins and check your emergency bag and supplies.
2. **Alert phase:** the cyclone is expected within about 48 hours. Prepare the house, water and medicines, and find out where you would go if told to evacuate.
3. **Alarm phase:** the cyclone will arrive in about 24 hours or is already arriving. If you are ordered to evacuate, go immediately. If not, stay in the smallest interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows and glass doors.
4. **Recovery phase:** the cyclone has passed. Go home only when the authorities say it is safe.

## After the cyclone
- Do not cross flooded streets or damaged bridges.
- Stay away from downed power lines and report any smell of gas.
- Never use a generator, a charcoal stove or a brazier indoors: carbon monoxide kills (see the NIDO Essentials guide on poisoning and carbon monoxide).

## Important
Phases and what they ask of you can change: always follow what the Civil Defense announces on radio and television. We could not consult an official Civil Defense website directly; the phases are described from its statements as published by EFE.
