In Puerto Rico, the emergency number is 9-1-1: police, fire and emergency medical services.

## Who warns
The National Weather Service office in San Juan issues hurricane and tropical storm watches and warnings. The Emergency Management and Disaster Administration Bureau (NMEAD) gives information on shelters, evacuation routes and safety measures. Without internet, a battery-powered radio keeps you informed.

## Watch or warning: the difference
- **Tropical storm watch:** tropical storm conditions (sustained winds of 39 to 73 mph) are possible in your area within 48 hours.
- **Tropical storm warning:** those conditions are expected in your area within 36 hours.
- **Hurricane watch:** hurricane conditions (sustained winds of 74 mph or more) are possible in your area.
- **Hurricane warning:** hurricane conditions are expected in your area. It is time to finish preparing and, if ordered, evacuate.

## What to do
- Before: find out whether you live in an evacuation zone, make a family plan and prepare your bag and supplies.
- During: evacuate immediately if ordered. If not, stay in a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows and glass doors.
- After: go home only when the authorities say it is safe. Avoid flooded roads and damaged bridges, and watch for downed power lines and gas leaks.
- Never use a portable generator inside the house or garage.
