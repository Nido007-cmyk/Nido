In Mexico, the emergency number is 911, the single number for the whole country since 2016-2017.

## The hurricane alert colors (SIAT-CT)
The Early Warning System for Tropical Cyclones uses five colors, from least to most danger: blue, green, yellow, orange and red. Civil Protection and the media announce them.

When the cyclone is **approaching**:
- **Blue:** more than 72 hours away. Stay informed.
- **Green:** 72 to 24 hours away. The authorities prepare shelters and staff. Check your bag and supplies.
- **Yellow:** 60 to 12 hours away. Shelters are made ready and boats stay in port. Have everything ready to leave.
- **Orange:** 36 to 6 hours away. Temporary shelters open, risk areas are evacuated and schools close. If you are in a risk area, go to the shelter.
- **Red:** impact in less than 18 hours. Stay sheltered.

When the cyclone is **moving away**, the colors go down from red to blue. Risks remain (floods, landslides, rising rivers): leave the shelter and go home only when the authorities say so, and keep water and food clean.

## The earthquake alert (SASMEX)
The Mexican Seismic Alert System warns of a strong earthquake before the shaking arrives, in the cities that receive it. Depending on the distance to the epicenter, the warning comes about 20 to 120 seconds ahead. The alert saves lives only if you know what to do: practice in the drills and follow the NIDO Essentials earthquake guide. On the coast, after a strong earthquake, move away from the sea in case of a tsunami.

## What to do in a hurricane
- If you are ordered to evacuate, go immediately.
- If not, stay in the smallest interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows.
- Never run a generator inside the house or garage.
