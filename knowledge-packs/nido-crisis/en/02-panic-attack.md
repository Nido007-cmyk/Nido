A panic attack is a sudden wave of very intense fear: the heart races, breathing is hard, hands shake, and there may be dizziness, sweating, or a feeling of losing control or that something terrible is about to happen. It usually peaks within a few minutes and lasts 5 to 20 minutes. It is terrifying, but a panic attack itself is not dangerous and it will pass.

Caution: if it is the first time, if there is chest pain, fainting, or breathlessness that does not ease, seek medical care or call emergency services: not everything that looks like panic is panic.

## If it's happening to someone else
- **Stay, and keep yourself calm.** Your calm gives them an anchor. Speak slowly, in short sentences.
- **Name what is happening:** "This is a panic attack. It's very unpleasant, but it will pass and I'm here." Don't argue with their sensations, and don't just say "calm down".
- **Ask what they need.** Some people want quiet company; others want to leave the noise or sit down. If they have had attacks before, ask what helped last time.
- **Breathe with them, without forcing it.** Breathe in slowly through the nose counting to 4, and out counting to 6, several times, at their pace. Don't have them breathe into a bag: that is not recommended.
- **Help them come back to the present (grounding).** Ask them to notice 5 things they can see, 4 they can touch, 3 they can hear, 2 they can smell and 1 they can taste; or to press their feet into the floor and describe where they are.
- **Don't let them drive** until they are fully well, and offer water and rest afterwards: an attack leaves the body exhausted.
- Go with them to seek medical help if the attacks keep happening, if they start avoiding places for fear of another one, or if the fear stops them living their life.

## If it's happening to you
1. **Remind yourself what it is:** "This is panic. It's a wave: it rises, peaks and falls. It won't harm me and it will pass."
2. **Stay where you are if it is safe.** Rushing away from the place often makes the fear of that place grow next time. Sit down if you need to.
3. **Make the out-breath longer.** Breathe in counting to 4 and out counting to 6, as if blowing slowly through a straw. Repeat for a few minutes; don't try to breathe "deeply and fast" — that increases dizziness.
4. **Anchor your senses:** quietly name what you see, hear and touch; notice the weight of your body on the chair and your feet on the floor.
5. **Stop fighting the sensations.** Let your heart beat fast without checking it every second: the less you fight, the sooner the wave falls.
6. **Afterwards,** rest, avoid caffeine and alcohol, and tell someone you trust. If attacks repeat, book an appointment with a professional: panic attacks can be treated and they do get better.

## What doesn't help
- Saying "it's nothing" or shaming the person in front of others.
- Giving alcohol "to relax" or medication that isn't theirs.
- Shaking them, slapping them, or throwing cold water on their face.
