The choking guide in the Essentials pack gives the full maneuver for adults, children and babies. This guide goes deeper into what changes with age, because the maneuver for a baby and the one for a child are not the same, and mixing them up causes harm.

## First: are they coughing loudly?
If you can see the object in their mouth, try to remove it, but do not poke blindly or repeatedly with your fingers: you could push the object further in and make it harder to remove. If they are coughing loudly, encourage them to carry on coughing and do not leave them. Use maneuvers only if the cough is not effective: it is silent, they cannot breathe in properly, or they cannot breathe. Shout for help right away.

## Baby under 1 year: back blows and chest thrusts
1. Sit down and lay your baby face down along your thigh or forearm, supporting their head and neck with your hand, with the head lower than the body.
2. Give up to 5 sharp back blows with the heel of one hand in the middle of the back, between the shoulder blades. Check after each blow whether the object has come out; you may not need all 5.
3. If it does not come out, lay your baby face up along your thighs and give up to 5 sharp chest thrusts in the middle of the chest, just below the nipple line. The AHA 2025 guidelines say to do these with the heel of one hand, compressing the chest by about a third.
4. Keep alternating back blows and chest thrusts until the object comes out or help arrives.

**Never give abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich maneuver) to a baby under 1 year**: their liver and internal organs are poorly protected, and pressure on the belly can injure them. In a baby, the pressure goes on the chest, not the abdomen.

## Child over 1 year: back blows and abdominal thrusts
1. Lay a small child face down on your lap as you would a baby; if that is not possible, support them in a forward-leaning position and give 5 sharp back blows from behind.
2. If that does not work, stand or kneel behind your child, place your arms under their arms and around their upper abdomen, clench your fist and place it between the navel and the ribs. Grasp that fist with your other hand and pull sharply inwards and upwards. Repeat up to 5 times, checking after each thrust.
3. Do not apply pressure to the lower ribcage: it can cause damage.
4. Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts.

## If it does not come out, or they lose consciousness
- Call your emergency number (put the phone on speaker) if the blockage does not come out after back blows and thrusts, and keep alternating until help arrives. Do not leave the child at any stage.
- If they become unconscious, put them on a firm, flat surface and start CPR for children and babies (guide in the Essentials pack).
- Even if the object comes out, get medical help: part of it may have been left behind, or your child may have been hurt by the procedure.

## Important
Based on the NHS and the AHA 2025 guidelines, in line with the choking guide in the Essentials pack. These maneuvers are learned by practicing in a pediatric first aid course; never practice them with force on a healthy child.
