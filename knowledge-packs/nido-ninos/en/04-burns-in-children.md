The burns guide in the Essentials pack works for any age. In a child, two things change: their skin is thinner, so the same hot liquid burns deeper, and cooling a large area can lower their whole body temperature. A spilled cup of hot drink is the classic accident.

## The first cooling: 20 minutes
1. Get your child away from the heat source, while keeping yourself safe.
2. Hold the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes, as soon as possible: starting it still helps up to 3 hours after the injury. Never use ice, iced water, or any creams or greasy substances like butter.
3. While cooling, remove any clothing or jewellery near the burnt area, including nappies, socks and shoes, but do not try to remove anything that is stuck to the burnt skin.
4. Keep the rest of your child warm with a blanket or coat: you cool the burn, not the child. Watch that they do not start shivering (risk of hypothermia, especially in babies and with large burns).
5. Once cooled, lay cling film over the burn (do not wrap it around), or use a clean plastic bag or a clean, non-fluffy cloth. Do not burst any blisters, and do not put toothpaste, oil, butter, egg or home remedies on it.

Note: the general guide in the Essentials pack says "at least 10 minutes". For children, follow this guide: 20 minutes, which is the current NHS advice.

## How to judge severity in a child
- **Size:** your child's hand, including the fingers, is roughly 1% of their body surface area. A burn larger than the size of your child's own hand needs urgent help.
- **Depth:** white or charred skin, blistering, or an area that does not hurt, point to a deep burn.
- **Place:** face, neck, hands, feet, genitals or over any joints are always serious areas.
- **Cause:** chemical and electrical burns are always serious, even when they look small.

## Go to emergency care now if...
- The burn is larger than your child's hand, or is deep, white or charred, or has blistered.
- It is on the face, neck, hands, feet, genitals or over a joint.
- It was caused by a chemical or by electricity.
- They inhaled smoke or have any difficulty breathing.
- Your child is under 2 years old.
- They have a temperature above 38 °C or become unwell following the burn.

## Home care is reasonable only if...
It is small (smaller than their hand), superficial (red skin, no blisters), not on any of the serious areas above, and your child is well. Keep it clean, do not burst blisters if they form, and if you are not sure what to do, get medical advice the same day. Small burns can take around 2 weeks to heal.

## Important
