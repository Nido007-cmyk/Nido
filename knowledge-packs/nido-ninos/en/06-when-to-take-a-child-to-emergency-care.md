The warning signs guide in the Essentials pack mixes adults, children and pregnancy. This is the children-only summary, organized by what you see: if any of these signs appears, do not wait for it to "go away on its own".

## The WHO's four general danger signs (IMCI)
In any sick child, check these four first. Each one, on its own, means urgent care:
- Not able to drink or breastfeed.
- Vomits everything.
- Has had convulsions in this illness.
- Is lethargic (very drowsy, limp) or unconscious, and does not wake up properly.

## Breathing
- Breathing very fast.
- The skin sinks in between the ribs or under the ribcage as they breathe.
- Noisy breathing while at rest, or grunting noises when breathing.
- Difficulty breathing, breathlessness, or blue, grey or very pale skin, lips or tongue.

## Fever by age
- Under 3 months with 38 °C / 100.4 °F or more: emergency care, always, even if they look well.
- 3 to 6 months with 39 °C or more: urgent advice the same day.
- At any age: a high temperature lasting 5 days or more, fever with a stiff neck, with a rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it, or with being bothered by light.

## Dehydration
- Nappies that are not very wet, or peeing rarely and dark.
- No tears when crying, sunken eyes, a dry mouth.
- In a baby, a sunken fontanelle (the soft spot on their head).
- Cannot drink, vomits everything, or is drowsy and hard to wake: that is emergency care, not home watching.

## Febrile seizures
Between 6 months and 5 years of age (the NHS extends this to 6 years), a fever can trigger a seizure: the body stiffens, jerks, and the child does not respond. They usually last 2 to 3 minutes, and it is rare for them to last more than 10 minutes.
- During: note the time, protect their head with something soft, and move dangerous objects away. Do not hold them down, and do not put anything in their mouth. Do not try to lower their temperature by undressing them or sponging them with cold water.
- After: put them on their side, in the recovery position.
- Call your emergency number or go to emergency care if it is their first seizure, it lasts more than 5 minutes, they have difficulty breathing, the stiffness and twitching are on only one side of the body, they have more than one seizure within 24 hours, or they are more sleepy than usual for more than 1 hour after it stops.
- After a first febrile seizure, your child needs to be checked in hospital even if it stopped. Fever medicines can make a child more comfortable, but they do not prevent these seizures.

## Other signs that do not wait
- Not responding like they normally do, not interested in feeding or normal activities, or extreme agitation and unstoppable crying.
- Unusually cold hands and feet.
- After a head bump: repeated vomiting, growing sleepiness, or any of the signs in this pack's bumps and falls guide.

## When you call
Say where you are, your child's age, what sign you see and since when. Do not hang up until you are told to. If they are having a seizure or are unconscious, do not drive them yourself: call your emergency number.
