The general NIDO Essentials guide already covers the basics of bites. This guide adds what is specific to the field: why each home remedy does harm, and why the only thing that saves lives is reaching a centre that has antivenom.

Worldwide, about 5.4 million people are bitten by snakes each year. The heaviest burden is in Africa, Asia and Latin America, and those who suffer most are farm workers and children: in a child the venom acts faster because the body is smaller.

## What to do, step by step
1. Move away from the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it: it can bite again. If you can, take a photo from a distance with your phone; it helps identify it.
2. Call the emergency number, or start right away towards the nearest health centre that stocks antivenom.
3. Keep the person still and calm. Movement speeds up the spread of venom.
4. Immobilize the bitten arm or leg as if it were a fracture, with a splint or a loose sling.
5. Remove rings, watches, bracelets and tight clothing from that limb: the area can swell a lot.
6. Cover the wound only with a clean, dry cloth.
7. Move the person with as little movement as possible: carried or by vehicle, not walking.

## What NEVER to do
- **Do NOT apply a tourniquet** or tie the limb: it cuts off circulation, can destroy tissue, and when released the venom rushes in all at once.
- **Do NOT cut the wound.**
- **Do NOT suck out the venom** by mouth or with suction devices: it does not remove it and adds infection.
- **Do NOT apply ice** to the area.
- **Do NOT try to heal the wound with herbs or chemicals.**
- **Do NOT give alcohol** or home remedies: they only delay reaching the hospital.

## What actually saves lives
Antivenom is the only effective antidote against snake venom, and only health workers can give it. So the goal in the field is not to "treat" the bite, but to buy time without doing harm: immobilize and get there.

Go to hospital even if the person feels well at first: some venoms are slow to show symptoms (paralysis, bleeding problems, kidney damage). Note the time of the bite and what the snake looked like if you saw it.

## Prevention in the field
- Boots and long trousers in brush, tall grass, or among rocks and firewood.
- A torch at night; never put your hand where you cannot see.
- Take care when lifting rocks or logs or reaching into holes.

## Important
General information based on WHO and PAHO. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
