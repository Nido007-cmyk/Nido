Most scorpion species in the world cause only local pain, but in some regions there are species whose venom can kill, above all children. This guide says where that happens and what to do.

## Where they are truly dangerous
- **Mexico:** it has the highest number of cases of any country. All of the country's medically important scorpions are in the genus *Centruroides*. The states with the highest mortality are Guerrero, Jalisco, Michoacán, Morelos, Nayarit, Sinaloa and Zacatecas, and the highest morbidity has been recorded in Jalisco, Guanajuato, Guerrero, Michoacán, Morelos and Nayarit.
- **Brazil:** the dangerous genus is *Tityus*. *Tityus serrulatus* is responsible for most severe cases and has spread to every region of the country; *Tityus stigmurus* is widely distributed in the Northeast, and about 85% of notifications are concentrated in the Northeast and Southeast. Most accidents happen inside the home and children up to 10 years old carry the highest risk of dying.
- **The rest of the Americas:** according to PAHO, scorpionism in the Americas occurs mainly in Mexico, northern South America and southeastern Brazil: the rule is the same, get to a health centre.

## Who is affected most
Young children are in the greatest danger: the same amount of venom weighs much more in a small body. In Mexico, children under 5 and people over 65, pregnant women and people with heart disease, asthma, diabetes or high blood pressure are considered high risk.

## What it feels like
- **Mild:** local pain, numbness and tingling, itching in the nose and throat, slight restlessness.
- **Moderate:** persistent crying in infants and small children, distress, headache, watering eyes, heavy saliva and nasal mucus, sneezing, a feeling of something stuck in the throat, difficulty swallowing, diarrhea, muscle pains.
- **Severe:** very high or very low blood pressure, fever, seizures, a heart beating very fast, very slowly or irregularly, bluish lips, difficulty breathing, unconsciousness. It can cause death.

## What to do
1. Go immediately to a doctor, medical unit or the nearest health centre. Do not wait to see if it "passes": with a child, act even if there is only pain.
2. Keep the person resting and calm on the way.
3. The specific treatment is antivenom: in Mexico it is called faboterápico antialacrán and in Brazil soro antiescorpiônico. Only health workers give it, and the sooner the better.

## What NEVER to do
- **Do NOT make incisions** at the sting site and **do NOT suck out the venom**.
- **Do NOT apply ice** to the sting site.
- **Do NOT use tourniquets**.
- **Do NOT give alcoholic drinks**.
- **Do NOT apply the antivenom at the sting site**, and no home remedies (garlic, milk, herbs): they do not work and delay care.
- **Do NOT try to catch the scorpion**: you could be stung again. If it is already dead and you can bring it without touching it, it may help identify it, but it is not necessary.

## Prevention at home
- Shake out clothes and shoes before using them; check the bed.
- Seal cracks, fit mesh on drains and draft strips on doors.
- Keep the house and yard free of rubble, stacked firewood and rubbish where scorpions hide.
- In the field, gloves and boots when moving rocks or wood.

## Important
General information based on Mexico's Secretariat of Health (Birmex, NOM-033-SSA2-2011) and Brazil's Ministry of Health. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
