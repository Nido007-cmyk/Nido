The poisoning guide in NIDO Essentials says what to do if someone swallows something toxic. This guide adds the field part: why with plants and mushrooms the typical mistake is identification, and why with the most dangerous mushrooms the symptoms arrive late.

## The golden rule
If there is no certain identification by a local expert, do not eat it. Not a wild plant, not a berry, not a mushroom.

- Poisonous mushrooms look very much like edible ones, even to experienced people: in the 2025-2026 California outbreak studied by the CDC, several people were poisoned by mushrooms that resembled ones they used to gather in their home countries.
- A photo, a phone app or "it looks like the usual one" is not a certain identification.
- Home tests do not work either: neither the colour when cut, nor whether animals eat it, nor taste or smell can tell you a mushroom is safe.

## The amatoxin mushroom trap
The mushrooms that cause most deaths worldwide (such as the death cap, *Amanita phalloides*) carry poisons called amatoxins:
- **Cooking, boiling or drying does NOT destroy the poison.**
- Symptoms arrive late, often more than 6 hours later (6 to 12 hours is typical): abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and diarrhea.
- Afterwards the person may feel better for a day, but the poison keeps damaging the liver in silence; after 2 to 3 days, liver and kidney failure can follow.
- That is why feeling better does not mean being safe, and waiting "to see if anything happens" loses the hours when treatment works best.

## If someone ate an unknown plant or mushroom
1. Call your poison control centre or local emergency number immediately, even if the person feels fine. With mushrooms, do not wait for symptoms.
2. **Do NOT induce vomiting**, and do not give milk, oil or remedies: follow only what the poison centre advises.
3. Keep a sample: leftovers of the plant or mushroom, or what remains of the meal. If there are no leftovers, take clear photos of other specimens from the same spot (of the whole mushroom: cap, gills, stem and base) and note which trees it was growing near. That lets an expert identify it.
4. Note what was eaten, how much, at what time, and who else ate the same: warn those people even if they feel well.
5. If there is vomiting, diarrhea, drowsiness or any symptom, go to emergency care and bring the sample or the photos.

## Children and the garden
- Most mushroom calls are about small children who bite a mushroom on the ground. Check the garden and play areas and remove mushrooms that come up.
- Teach children that no plant, berry or mushroom from the field or garden is tasted without an adult who truly knows it.
- Watch house and ornamental plants too: some are toxic when chewed. If a child bites one, call the poison centre, with the plant identified if you can.

## What NEVER to do
- **Do NOT eat** a wild mushroom or plant identified "by eye", from a photo or by an app.
- **Do NOT wait for symptoms** before calling if an unknown mushroom was eaten.
- **Do NOT throw the leftovers away**: the sample or photo can change the treatment.
- **Do NOT assume someone is safe** because they feel better the next day after a mushroom meal: check again if symptoms started late.

## Important
General information based on the CDC and the BCCDC. Your country's poison control centre should guide each case.
