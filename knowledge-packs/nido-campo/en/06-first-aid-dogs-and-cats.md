First aid is not a substitute for veterinary care: its job is to keep your animal alive until it reaches the clinic. A pet in pain or frightened may bite even if it is usually gentle: approach calmly, keep your face away from its mouth and, if it threatens to bite and is not vomiting, you can apply a makeshift muzzle with a bandage or cloth.

## Choking
Signs: difficulty breathing, pawing at the mouth, choking sounds when breathing or coughing, and possibly blue-tinged lips or tongue.
1. If your pet can still breathe, keep it calm and seek veterinary care immediately.
2. If you can do so without risk, open its mouth and look for an object. If you see one, try to remove it gently with your fingers or tweezers, without pushing it further down.
3. For cats and small dogs, if fingers do not clear it: gently pick the pet up by its thighs and swing it from side to side. If that does not help, apply forward pressure to the abdomen just behind the ribcage.
4. For larger dogs, use the Heimlich maneuver: if the dog is standing, place your arms around its belly, make a fist on the soft spot under the ribcage, and push up and forward. If the dog is lying down, place one hand on its back and use the other to squeeze the abdomen upward.
5. Even if the object comes out, take your pet to the veterinarian: the throat may be injured.

## Bleeding
1. Use a clean cloth or towel to apply firm pressure directly over the bleeding site.
2. Avoid checking whether the bleeding has stopped until you have applied pressure for at least 3 minutes.
3. If blood soaks through the cloth, **do NOT remove it**: add towels on top (removing it disturbs the clots that have formed) and keep pressing.
4. If the bleeding is severe, immediately take your pet to the closest veterinary hospital while keeping pressure.
5. If a foreign object is stuck in the wound, do not try to remove it: if necessary, carefully trim it leaving 3 to 6 inches (8 to 15 cm) sticking out, before seeking veterinary care.

## Heatstroke
Signs: rapid breathing or heavy panting, excessive drooling, weakness or drowsiness, confusion, vomiting or diarrhea, and abnormally coloured, dry or sticky gums. Never leave an animal in a car in the sun: within minutes it can be fatal.
1. Take your pet to the closest veterinary hospital as soon as possible, and begin cooling right away.
2. If your pet is in direct sunlight, move it to a shaded area or a comfortably cool indoor environment.
3. Place towels wetted with room-temperature water gently around its neck, in the armpits and in the groin area. Rewet and replace them every few minutes.
4. If a fan is available, use it to blow cool air onto your pet.
5. **Do NOT put your pet in a tub of cool or cold water**: it can make its condition worse. **Do NOT use ice.**

## Suspected poisoning
Typical causes: chocolate, human medicines, rat poison, antifreeze, some plants and cleaning products.
1. Immediately call your veterinarian, local emergency veterinary hospital, or an animal poison control hotline. Have the product container available for reference.
2. **Do NOT try to induce vomiting or give any medication** unless directed to do so by a veterinarian or poison control centre.
3. Collect any vomited or chewed material, seal it in a plastic bag and take it with you to the veterinary hospital.
4. Note what was eaten, how much, and when.

## Transport to the veterinarian
- Call ahead if you can, so they can be ready when you arrive.
- While transporting your injured pet, keep it confined in a small area to reduce the risk of additional injury: pet carriers work well; for large dogs, use a board, sled, blanket or something similar as a stretcher.
- If you can safely stabilize an injury by bandaging or splinting it, do so; if you are uncertain, leave it to the veterinarian: a poorly applied bandage can do more harm than good.
- Save the numbers of your veterinarian and the nearest emergency hospital in your phone before you need them.

## What NEVER to do
- **Do NOT muzzle your pet if it is vomiting**: a makeshift muzzle is only for a pet that threatens to bite and is not vomiting.
- **Do NOT push a choking object further down** while trying to remove it with your fingers or tweezers.
- **Do NOT remove a blood-soaked cloth**: add towels on top and keep pressing.
- **Do NOT remove an object stuck** in a wound.
- **Do NOT put your pet in a tub of cool or cold water** if it has heatstroke, and **do NOT use ice**.
- **Do NOT try to induce vomiting or give any medication** if you suspect poisoning, unless directed to do so by a veterinarian or poison control centre.
- **Never leave an animal in a car in the sun.**

## Important
General information based on the AVMA. In any real emergency, the destination is always the veterinary clinic.
