Rabies is almost unique among diseases: once symptoms appear it is fatal in practically 100% of cases, but before that it can be completely prevented with wound washing and vaccine. That is why this guide stands apart from the general bite guide: what decides the outcome here is time, and which animal it was.

## First: wash, and do not wait
1. Wash the wound immediately with soap and water for at least 15 minutes. It is the most important step: washing flushes the virus out of the wound.
2. Go the same day to a health centre to assess rabies vaccine. Do not wait to see whether the animal "looks sick", and do not let days pass.
3. Depending on the type of contact, health workers will give the vaccine and, in severe cases, rabies immunoglobulin as well.

The virus travels along the nerves towards the brain, and that journey takes weeks or months. The vaccine works during that interval, but nobody can know how long yours will last: that is why it cannot wait. Once there are symptoms (fever, tingling at the wound, agitation, fear of water), no treatment can stop it.

## Which contacts count (per WHO)
- **No risk:** touching or feeding an animal, or licks on healthy, unbroken skin. Wash the skin and nothing more.
- **Risk:** nibbling of uncovered skin, minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding. Wash and vaccinate immediately.
- **Severe risk:** single or multiple bites or scratches that break the skin, saliva on mucous membranes (eyes, mouth) or on broken skin, and **any direct contact with bats**. Wash, immediate vaccination and immunoglobulin if indicated.

## The special case of the bat
A bat's teeth are so small that its bite may not be felt or leave a mark. Because of that:
- Never touch a bat with your hands.
- If it bites or scratches you, or its saliva gets into your eyes, mouth or a wound: wash and get medical help right away.
- If you wake up to find a bat in your room, or one turns up in the bedroom of a small child or of someone who was asleep and cannot say whether they touched it, treat it as a possible contact: talk that same day to public health or a health centre.
- If possible, have animal control capture it without touching it so it can be tested: if the test is negative, vaccine may not be needed. Do not release it before asking.

In the Americas, where dog rabies is largely controlled, blood-feeding bats are now the main source of human rabies. In Africa and Asia, by contrast, dogs cause 99% of cases.

## The animal you can observe, and the one you cannot
- **A known, healthy dog, cat or ferret that bites:** in coordination with public health, the animal is confined and observed for 10 days; if it stays healthy — even if its vaccination is up to date — the health team will decide whether to complete or stop vaccination. Do not decide alone: still start the consultation.
- **A stray, wild or fleeing animal:** observation is impossible. Treat it as a real risk: wash and get vaccine assessment without delay.
- **A bat:** it is not observed; it is tested, or the risk is assumed.
- Do not try to capture a wild or stray animal that bit you: call animal control.

## What NEVER to do
- **Do NOT wait** for symptoms before seeking care.
- **Do NOT close or stitch the wound** on your own before assessment, and **do NOT apply home remedies** (herbs, earth, pastes): wash only with soap and water.
- **Do NOT kill or release the bat** from the room without asking: testing it can spare you the vaccine, or confirm you need it.

## Prevention
- Vaccinate your dogs and cats against rabies and keep their records up to date: vaccinating dogs is the most effective way to end human rabies.
- Keep your distance from wild animals, injured animals, or animals behaving oddly (active by day when they are nocturnal, unable to fly, letting people approach).
- Teach children never to touch unknown animals or bats, and to tell an adult if one touches them.

## Important
General information based on WHO and the CDC. The decision to vaccinate belongs to health staff: your part is to wash for 15 minutes and arrive the same day.
