Almost all spiders are harmless to people and do not seek to bite: they bite when trapped against the skin, for example inside clothing or when moving firewood. Two of them can make you seriously ill: the black widow and the recluse (violin) spider.

## The black widow
- Recognized by the red hourglass marking on the underside of its abdomen. It lives across North and South America, in undisturbed places: woodpiles, fences, rubble, outdoor toilets.
- Its bite leaves two small fang marks. Its venom attacks the nerves: pain starts at the bite and can spread to the chest, abdomen or the whole body, with muscle cramps and stiffness.

## The recluse or violin spider
- Brown, with a dark violin-shaped marking on its head, and it has six eyes (most spiders have eight). It lives in the midwestern and southern United States.
- It cannot bite unless pressed against the skin. The bite may feel like a stinging pinch and a small white blister usually develops.
- Its venom can destroy skin tissue (necrosis): the lesion grows, turns reddish to purplish and needs professional medical care. Do not judge severity by the first day: these lesions evolve.

## Symptoms to watch for
- Pain radiating from the bite, muscle pain or cramping.
- A reddish to purplish area or a growing blister.
- Sweating, fever or chills.
- Difficulty breathing.
- Headache, nausea or vomiting.
- Restlessness and high blood pressure.

## What to do
1. Stay calm. Identify the type of spider if you can do so safely: it helps medical treatment. Do not risk another bite to catch it.
2. Wash the bite area with soap and water.
3. Apply a cloth dampened with cold water or filled with ice to reduce swelling.
4. Elevate the bite area if possible.
5. Seek professional medical attention, especially if you believe it was a black widow or a recluse, if the person is a child, pregnant or older, or if any of the symptoms above appear.

## What NEVER to do
- **Do NOT attempt to remove venom**: not by sucking, cutting or squeezing.
- **Do NOT self-treat** the lesion with home remedies.
- **Do NOT wait at home** if the lesion grows, turns purplish or ulcerates: recluse necrosis is best treated when assessed early.

## Prevention
- Inspect or shake out clothing, shoes, towels and gloves that have been stored or left on the ground.
- Long sleeves, gloves and boots when handling stacked or undisturbed materials.
- Store clothing and equipment in tightly closed bags; reduce rubble and tall grass around the house.
- Keep tetanus boosters up to date (every 10 years): spider bites can become infected.
