Most vehicle deaths in floods happen because someone decided to cross. Moving water is far stronger than it looks, and it hides the ground: the road may no longer be underneath. If a car falls into a river or a lake in a crash, follow the road traffic crash guide in NIDO Essentials; this guide is about floodwater.

## Never cross
- **Turn around, don't drown.** Do not walk, swim or drive through flood waters.
- Just six inches of moving water can knock an adult off their feet, and most vehicles begin to lose contact with the road in that same amount.
- One foot of moving water can sweep a car away, and 18 to 24 inches will carry away SUVs and pickup trucks too.
- Never drive around barricades or closure signs: they are there because the road is unsafe, however shallow the water looks.
- Stay off bridges over fast-moving water: the current can wash a bridge away without warning.
- A cancelled or delayed trip is worth it: if your route crosses flooded areas, cancel or delay the trip and head for higher ground.

## If the water traps you in the car
- If your car is trapped in rapidly moving water, **stay inside the car**.
- If water is rising inside the car, **get on the roof** and signal for help from there.
- Do not try to cross a current on foot that reaches your knees: it can knock you down and carry you away.
- Call the emergency number as soon as you can do so without putting yourself in more danger, and give your location as well as you know it.

## Afterwards
- Do not drive in the area again until the authorities say it is safe, and avoid driving except in emergencies.
- Keep away from flood water on foot too: it can be contaminated and can hide downed power lines and debris.
