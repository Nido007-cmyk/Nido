A breakdown is not a crash: nobody has collided and nobody may be hurt. The danger is different: a stopped car is an obstacle, and the people who get out of it are exposed to traffic. This guide is for that moment. If there has been a crash with injuries, follow the road traffic crash guide in NIDO Essentials.

## 1. Stop somewhere safe
- Turn on your hazard lights as soon as you notice the problem.
- Slow down gradually and move the car off the travelled road whenever you can: a wide shoulder, a lay-by, a rest area or a nearby exit.
- Do not stop in a lane, on a bend, over a crest or in a tunnel if you can avoid it.

## 2. Make the car visible
- Mark the stopped car with the device your country requires: in Spain, the V16 luminous beacon; in other countries, a warning triangle or an equivalent device.
- Leave the hazard lights on so others can see you, in daylight too.

## 3. Get out on the safe side
- Put on the reflective vest **before** you get out.
- Leave the vehicle on the side away from traffic, and help passengers out on that same side. Children always exit on the safe side.
- If there is no safe place off the road, or getting out is more dangerous than staying in, stay inside with seat belts on until it is safe to leave.

## 4. Wait off the road
- Wait for help in a safe place: behind the guardrail or off the road, never on the shoulder or between lanes.
- Do not attempt repairs next to moving traffic: no changing a wheel on the traffic side, no getting under the car on the shoulder.
- Do not cross the motorway on foot to look for help.

## 5. Call for help with your location
- Call your roadside assistance or, if there is danger, the emergency number. In Mexico, Ángeles Verdes provide free mechanical help and roadside assistance on 078; in Brazil, the Federal Highway Police handle emergencies on federal highways on 191.
- Say exactly where you are: road, kilometre marker, direction of travel and a nearby landmark. Your phone's location helps.
- Keep your insurer's and assistance numbers in your phone before you travel.

## Important
Based on the DGT (Spain) and NHTSA (United States). The warning device and the assistance number change by country: check the ones for your route before you set off.
