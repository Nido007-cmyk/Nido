A prepared car turns a breakdown into a wait. Here is the list for a road trip: the kit for holding out and calling for help, and the documents you will be asked for if you are stopped or something happens.

## The emergency kit
According to NHTSA, even a well-maintained vehicle can break down, so carry a kit with:
- A cell phone and a car charger (and a power bank, as Ready.gov suggests).
- A first aid kit and any medicines you need.
- A flashlight.
- Jumper cables.
- Nonperishable food and drinking water.
- Emergency blankets and warm coats.
- Flares or warning devices, where these are used.

In winter, add: a small shovel, a broom and an ice scraper, sand or kitty litter for traction if you get stuck, and spare warm clothing.

## What your country requires
- The warning device your rules require (in Spain, the V16 luminous beacon; in other countries, warning triangles) and the reflective vest kept inside the cabin, so you can put it on before getting out.
- A spare wheel at the right pressure, or a puncture repair kit, and the tools to use them.
- Check the kit every six months and replace anything expired: water, food, batteries and medicines.

## The documents
The list changes by country. In Spain, the DGT requires you to carry three documents at all times, and in force:
- The driver's driving licence.
- The vehicle's registration document (permiso de circulación).
- The vehicle's technical inspection card (ITV).

And anywhere, on a long trip it is worth carrying as well:
- Your insurance policy or proof of insurance and your insurer's roadside assistance phone number, also saved in your phone.
- A blank friendly accident statement form (or your insurer's app) in the glovebox.
- A copy of the documents in a waterproof bag or on your phone, in case the originals are lost or soaked.
- If you cross into another country, check beforehand what extra documents it demands: some require an international insurance certificate (the former Green Card) and an international driving permit.

## Before you set off
- Check tyre pressures and tread (including the spare), oil, brake fluid and washer levels, lights and battery.
- Keep the fuel tank as full as you reasonably can, and tell someone your route and your expected arrival time.

## Important
Based on NHTSA (United States), the DGT (Spain) and Ready.gov (FEMA). The mandatory documents listed are Spain's, according to the DGT; in another country, check yours before you travel.
