Fog, heavy rain, ice and dust all take away the same two things: visibility and grip. The first response is the same too: less speed, more distance and the right lights. And when you cannot see far enough to stop within what you can see, the right decision is to stop completely, off the road.

## The general rule
- **Slow down and increase your following distance.** In rain, braking distances grow; on a wet road the DGT recommends twice the distance you would keep on a dry one.
- Never drive faster than the speed that lets you stop within the distance you can actually see.
- No sudden braking, lane changes or steering. Avoid overtaking.

## Fog
- Turn on low-beam headlights and fog lamps. **Do not use high beams**: they reflect off the fog and create glare.
- Use the DGT's **3V rule**: Visibility = Velocity = Vehicle ahead. If you can see 50 metres, do not exceed 50 km/h and keep about 50 metres from the car in front.
- Use the painted markings on the right edge of the road as a guide and watch the brake lights of the vehicle ahead.
- If the fog is so thick that you cannot continue safely, pull completely off the road, turn on your hazard flashers and wait for it to lift. Avoid stopping on the shoulder if you can: in fog it is especially dangerous.

## Heavy rain
- Low beams on, wipers and defroster working.
- Touch the brakes gently from time to time to dry moisture off the pads and check that they respond.
- Beware of hydroplaning: if the car starts to "float", do not brake suddenly, to avoid skidding.
- If the rain blinds you, stop carefully **off the road** until it eases.

## Ice and snow
- Learn to spot it: ice on the windshield, high humidity and bright sunshine, whitish patches of frost or ice beside the road.
- Slow down gradually and keep your speed very low. Avoid sudden braking, acceleration or steering, and do not overtake.
- In winter, increase your following distance (Ready.gov suggests going from 3-4 seconds to 5-6 seconds) and consider postponing the trip if the roads are in poor shape.

## Dust storm
- If you can, **do not enter** the dust area.
- If it reaches you: pull your vehicle off the pavement as far as possible, stop, **turn off all lights**, set the emergency brake and take your foot off the brake pedal. Lit tail lights can lead another driver to follow them, thinking you are moving, and hit you from behind.
- Never stop on the travelled portion of the road. If you cannot pull off, proceed at a speed suitable for the visibility, with lights on, until you find a safe place.
- Wait inside the car, belted, until it passes: dust storms usually last only a few minutes.

## When to stop altogether
Pull off the road in a suitable place when heavy precipitation or an extreme phenomenon (snow, ice, wind, fog) makes driving difficult and dangerous, and wait until conditions end before resuming. Tell your destination you will be late.
