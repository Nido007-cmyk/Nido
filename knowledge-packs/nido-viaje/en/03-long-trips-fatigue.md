On a long trip the risk is not only the car: it is the driver's tiredness and the heat inside a parked vehicle. Both are prevented before you set off and with real stops, not tricks.

## Before you leave
- Get a good night's sleep: seven to eight hours. Too little sleep is the root cause of drowsy driving, and no coffee replaces it.
- Plan stops and, if you can, take turns with another rested driver.
- Avoid driving during the peak sleepiness periods: between midnight and 6 a.m. and in the late afternoon.
- Check your medications: some cause drowsiness. Do not drink alcohol before or during the trip.

## Fatigue and microsleeps
- Warning signs: repeated yawning, heavy eyelids, constant blinking, itchy eyes, endless shifting in the seat, neck or back pain, drifting over the line or onto the rumble strip.
- **Stop every 2 hours** even if you do not feel tired: get out, walk, breathe and stretch.
- Coffee alone is not enough: if you are truly sleepy you can have "microsleeps" of four or five seconds without noticing; at 55 mph that is more than 100 yards travelled asleep.
- What works is sleep: pull over in a safe, lighted place, such as a designated rest stop, and take a short 20-minute nap. Cold air, loud music or opening a window only give a passing feeling of alertness.

## Children in the car
- They ride in the right car seat or booster for their age and size, in the back seat, on every trip.
- **Never leave a child alone in a car, not even for a minute**, not with the engine running and not with a window down: the inside heats up very quickly, and a child's body temperature rises three to five times faster than an adult's. Rolling the windows down or parking in the shade does little to change the interior temperature.
- Build the habit of looking in the back seat before you lock: leave your bag, phone or a stuffed animal next to the child seat so you always have to look.
- Keep the car locked and the keys out of reach: a child who climbs in to play can become trapped.
- If you see a child alone in a car who is unresponsive or in distress, call the emergency number immediately and help them out as fast as you can.

## Pets in the car
- **Never leave people or pets in a closed car on a warm day**: Ready.gov's rule protects both.
- At stops, take your pet out with you, into the shade, with fresh water. Hot asphalt burns their paws too.
- Carry their water, food and anything they need in the trip kit; during a long wait after a breakdown or in a jam you will need it.
