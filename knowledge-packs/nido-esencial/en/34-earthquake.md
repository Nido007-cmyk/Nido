## Before
- Fix tall furniture, shelves and water heaters to the wall. Don't put heavy objects above beds.
- In every room, identify a safe spot: under a sturdy table or against an interior wall, away from windows.
- Know how to shut off the gas. Keep your go-bag and shoes by the bed.

## During: DROP, COVER AND HOLD ON
1. **Drop** to your hands and knees before the shaking knocks you down.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with one arm. If you can, get under a sturdy table; if not, next to an interior wall.
3. **Hold on** to the table (or cover with both arms) until the shaking stops.
- Don't run outside during the shaking: most injuries happen from falling objects while getting out. Don't stand in a doorway.
- In bed: stay there, lie face down and cover your head with the pillow.
- In a wheelchair: lock the wheels, bend forward and cover your head.
- Outdoors: move away from buildings, power lines and trees; drop and cover.
- In a car: pull over in an open place (not under bridges or power lines) and stay inside with your seatbelt on.
- On the coast: if the shaking is strong or long, as soon as it stops, go to high ground (see tsunami).

## After
1. Expect aftershocks: each time, drop, cover and hold on again.
2. Check whether you and others are hurt. Put on shoes (broken glass).
3. If you smell gas, shut off the main valve, open windows and get out. Don't turn on lights, lighters or phones inside.
4. If the building is damaged, leave carefully by the stairs, never the elevator.
5. If you are trapped: cover your mouth with cloth, don't light a fire, tap on a pipe or wall or use a whistle so you can be found. Shout only as a last resort so you don't inhale dust.
6. Use the phone only for emergencies. Send text messages or use NIDO to reach your family.
7. Listen to the radio and follow the authorities' instructions.

## Important
