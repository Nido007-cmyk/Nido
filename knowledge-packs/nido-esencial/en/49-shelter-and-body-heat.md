In cold, wind or rain, shelter is more urgent than water or food.

## How the body loses heat
Through cold ground, wind, wet clothing and breathing. So:
- **Insulate yourself from the ground**: this matters most. Put a thick layer underneath of branches, dry leaves, grass, backpacks or cardboard (at least 10 to 15 cm / 4 to 6 inches when compressed).
- **Protect yourself from wind and rain.**
- **Stay dry**: take off wet clothes if you can dry or change them. Avoid sweating.
- **Layers of clothing**: several thin layers are warmer than one thick one. Cover your head and neck.

## Choosing the spot
- Near water but not in a riverbed, dry wash or gully (flash flood risk).
- Away from dead trees or branches that could fall, unstable slopes and avalanche zones.
- Visible from afar or from the air if you're waiting for rescue.
- Use what is there: a large fallen tree, a rock, a car, a building.

## Simple shelters
- **With a tarp or plastic sheet**: tie it between two trees with a rope and fix the sides to the ground, as a tent or a lean-to angled against the wind.
- **Branches and leaves**: a long pole resting on a stump or tree, branches leaning on the sides, and a thick layer of leaves, grass or leafy branches on top. Keep it small: just room for you, so it warms up sooner.
- **In snow**: a hollow under a tree with low branches, or a cave in a snowbank, with a ventilation hole.
- **Foil blanket**: use it as a layer against wind or to reflect heat from a fire.

## In extreme heat
- Find shade and rest in the hottest hours; move at dawn and dusk.
- Don't lie directly on hot ground.
- Cover your skin with light clothing, and your head.
