## Measures that work against almost all infections
- Wash your hands often with soap and water.
- Cough and sneeze into your elbow or a tissue you throw away at once.
- Ventilate closed spaces.
- If you are sick, stay home and avoid contact with vulnerable people.
- Wear a mask when the authorities recommend it, in crowded indoor places, or when caring for someone with a respiratory illness.
- Get vaccinated according to official recommendations.

## Prepare
- Keep food, water, regular medicines and hygiene products for 2 weeks in case you must stay home.
- Have a thermometer, paracetamol and oral rehydration salts.
- Plan who will care for children or older people if you get sick.

## Caring for a sick person at home
- If possible, one caregiver and a separate room.
- Masks for both when close, handwashing after every contact.
- Wash their clothes, dishes and surfaces with hot water and soap.
- Watch for warning signs (see that guide): trouble breathing, confusion, dehydration.

## Water- and mosquito-borne diseases (after floods)
- Cholera and other diarrheal diseases: safe water, latrines, handwashing; rehydrate early.
- Dengue, malaria, Zika: get rid of standing water (buckets, tires, plant pots), use repellent, bed nets and long sleeves.
- Leptospirosis: avoid walking barefoot in floodwater, cover wounds.

## Reliable information
Follow health authorities and the WHO. Be wary of miracle cures and alarmist messages without a source.
