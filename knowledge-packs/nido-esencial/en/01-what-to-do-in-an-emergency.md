In any emergency, follow three steps in this order: PROTECT, CALL and CARE. Stay calm: a few seconds to think prevent mistakes.

## 1. Protect
- Look around before you go closer: traffic, fire, power lines, gas, water, collapsing structures, violence.
- Do not go in if it puts you in danger. One more injured person helps nobody.
- If it is safe and there is immediate danger (fire, traffic), move the person away. Otherwise, do not move them.
- Put on gloves if you have them. If not, use a clean plastic bag as a barrier.

## 2. Call
- Call your country's emergency number (for example 911 in the Americas, 112 in Europe and many other countries). From almost any mobile phone, 112 works even without credit.
- Say calmly: where you are (address, landmarks), what happened, how many people are hurt and how they are (are they responding? breathing?).
- Do not hang up until you are told to: they will give you instructions.
- If other people are around, ask a specific person to call ("You, in the blue shirt, call 911") and another to find a defibrillator (AED).
- No signal: send someone for help, or use a radio, distress signals or NIDO messages to nearby contacts.

## 3. Care: what to check first
1. Do they respond? Speak loudly and tap their shoulders: "Can you hear me?"
2. Are they breathing normally? Look at the chest, listen and feel for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is NOT normal breathing.
3. Are they bleeding heavily? Severe bleeding is treated at once with direct pressure.
- Not responding and not breathing normally: start CPR (see the CPR guide).
- Not responding but breathing normally: recovery position, and keep checking their breathing.
- Responding: ask what happened, what hurts, and whether they take medicines or have allergies. Reassure them and do not leave them alone.

## What NOT to do
- Do not give food or drink to someone who is injured, dizzy or drowsy.
- Do not move someone who may have hurt their neck or back (falls, road crashes) unless there is immediate danger.
- Do not give medicines that are not theirs, except those in these guides (for example aspirin for chest pain) and only if there is no reason not to.

## Important
Based on Red Cross and AHA guidelines. Not a substitute for a first aid course: take one, many are free (your national Red Cross, online courses). Whenever you can, call your emergency number.
