Preparing in advance is what saves the most lives in a disaster. Do it as a family, in one afternoon.

## 1. Know your risks
What can happen where you live? Earthquakes, floods, hurricanes, wildfires, heat waves, volcanoes, power cuts... Ask your local civil protection or emergency management office. Find out whether your home is in a flood or tsunami zone.

## 2. Choose meeting points
- One right outside your home (for example, the corner) in case of fire.
- One outside your neighborhood in case you can't return.
- Know your area's evacuation routes and shelters, with two different ways to get there.

## 3. Communication plan
- A paper list of important phone numbers (batteries run out).
- A contact outside your region: after a disaster it is sometimes easier to call far away than nearby. Everyone tells them how they are.
- Text messages often get through when calls don't.
- Agree how you will communicate if there is no network: NIDO works over Bluetooth between paired phones, without internet or signal. Pair your family's phones beforehand.

## 4. Who needs extra help
Babies, older people, people with disabilities or illnesses, pets. Prepare their medicines, equipment and how you will move them. Know your neighbors: they may help you or need help.

## 5. At home
- Know how to shut off gas, water and electricity (and keep the tool at hand).
- Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and a fire extinguisher.
- Fix tall, heavy furniture to the wall.
- Copies of important documents (ID, insurance, prescriptions, deeds) in a waterproof bag, plus a digital copy.
- Some cash: after a disaster, ATMs and cards may not work.

## 6. Kits
One emergency go-bag per person and supplies at home for at least 3 days (better 2 weeks). See the emergency bag guide.

## 7. Practice
Do a drill once a year: get out of the house, reach the meeting point, use the communication plan. Teach children how to call the emergency number and their address.

## Important
