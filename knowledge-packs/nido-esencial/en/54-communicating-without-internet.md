After a disaster, phone and internet networks often go down or get overloaded. Have several ways ready.

## With the phone
- **Text messages (SMS)** rather than calls: they need less signal and don't overload the network.
- Call the emergency number even without coverage from your provider: emergency calls can use any available network.
- Keep calls short. Keep the line free for emergencies.
- Update your status in a single message to your out-of-area contact; they pass it on to the others.
- Save battery: power-saving mode, low brightness, close apps, airplane mode when not in use. Charge with a power bank, the car or a solar charger.

## With NIDO (no internet or carrier)
- NIDO can send encrypted messages between phones with NIDO **paired by QR code**, over Bluetooth (or local Wi-Fi as an alternative), without internet or servers.
- **Pair your family's and neighbors' phones before the emergency**: pairing is done in person.
- Bluetooth range is short (usually tens of meters, less through walls). If the other phone is far away, the message is kept encrypted and delivered on its own when the two phones are close again.
- NIDO's assistant works offline and can answer using these guides.

## Without a phone
- **Battery or hand-crank radio**: the most reliable way to get official alerts.
- **Walkie-talkies**: useful for a family or group at short range; agree on a channel.
- **Amateur (ham) radio operators**: in many disasters they keep emergency communications going.
- **Paper notes**: leave a visible note at home or at the meeting point saying who is OK, where you're going and when.
- **Message boards and meeting points** at shelters.

## Finding missing relatives
The Red Cross and Red Crescent run a **Restoring Family Links** service to reunite families separated by disasters and conflicts. Ask at your local Red Cross or at the shelter.
