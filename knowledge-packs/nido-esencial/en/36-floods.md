Floods are the most common disaster. Most deaths happen in vehicles and while trying to cross water.

## Before
- Find out whether your area floods and the routes to high ground.
- Move valuables, documents and chemicals to upper floors.
- Keep your go-bag ready and your phone charged.

## When there is a warning
- If you are told to evacuate, do it at once.
- If there is time: switch off electricity and gas at the main (don't touch anything electrical if you are wet or standing in water).
- Go to high ground. Avoid rivers, gullies, dry riverbeds and underpasses.

## Never cross moving water
- **Turn around.** About 15 cm (6 inches) of moving water can knock an adult down. About 30 cm (1 foot) can carry away a car, and 60 cm (2 feet) an SUV.
- You don't know how deep it is or whether the road is still there underneath.
- If water rises around your car, get out and go to high ground. If the car is in moving water, stay inside and climb onto the roof if water comes in.
- If you are trapped in a building, go to the highest level. Don't climb into a closed attic (you may get trapped): go onto the roof only if needed to signal for help.

## After
- Don't return home until it is allowed.
- Floodwater can contain sewage, chemicals, live wires and animals. Avoid touching it; if you do, wash well. Cover wounds.
- Don't drink tap water until told it's safe: boil or treat it.
- Throw away food that touched floodwater.
- Air out and dry the house within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.
- Don't use gasoline generators or pumps indoors (carbon monoxide).
- Photograph the damage for insurance.
