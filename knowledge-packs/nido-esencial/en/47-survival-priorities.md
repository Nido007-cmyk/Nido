In a survival situation, the biggest dangers are panic and spending energy on things that don't matter. Put your priorities in order.

## The rule of threes (approximate)
A person can last roughly:
- 3 minutes without air (or with uncontrolled severe bleeding).
- 3 hours without shelter in cold, wind or rain, or in extreme heat.
- 3 days without water.
- 3 weeks without food.
So the order is usually: **safety and first aid → shelter and body temperature → signals so you can be found → water → food**.

## Stop and think (STOP)
- **S**it down and breathe. Don't rush off aimlessly.
- **T**hink: what happened? what are the dangers? does anyone know where you are?
- **O**bserve: what you have, what's around you, how long until dark, what the weather is doing.
- **P**lan, then act.

## Mindset
- The will to survive is your best tool. Set small goals: "now I build the shelter", "now I look for water".
- Save energy and sweat. Rest in the hottest hours.
- In a group: stay together, share tasks, look after the weakest.

## What saves lives before you go
- Always tell someone where you are going and when you'll be back.
- Carry water, warm clothing, a flashlight, a whistle, a first aid kit, a foil blanket, a power bank and some food, even for a short trip.

## Important
General information based on recommendations from the Red Cross, Ready.gov and search-and-rescue organizations. Call your emergency number as soon as you can.
