## Asthma attack
Signs: wheezing, coughing, a tight chest, trouble speaking full sentences.
1. Help the person sit upright, leaning slightly forward. Loosen tight clothing.
2. Move them away from the trigger (smoke, dust, cold, animals).
3. Help them use their reliever inhaler (usually blue). It works better with a spacer; if there is none, one can be improvised with a cut plastic bottle.
4. Reassure them: fear makes breathing worse. Breathe slowly together.

## Call the emergency number if...
- They have no inhaler or it doesn't help.
- They cannot speak, their lips or nails turn bluish, or they are very tired or confused.
- It is the first time it has happened.

## Other breathing difficulty
- Sit them comfortably, loosen clothing and help them take their medicine if they have it.
- If they are very short of breath, breathing very slowly, or their level of consciousness changes, call the emergency number.
- Children with a "barking" cough and noisy breathing (croup): let them take the position in which they breathe best and have a doctor see them; if breathing is very hard, call the emergency number.

## Panic attack or hyperventilation
Fast breathing with tingling in the hands and mouth and intense fear. Speak calmly, help them breathe slowly (in for 4 seconds, out for 6). Do not use a paper bag. If it is not clear it is panic (chest pain, asthma, diabetes), treat it as a medical problem and call.

## Important
Based on the Canadian Red Cross, AHA and American Red Cross guidelines. Call your emergency number.
