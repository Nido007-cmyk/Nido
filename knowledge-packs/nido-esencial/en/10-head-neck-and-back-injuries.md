## When to suspect a spinal injury
- Road crash, being hit by a vehicle, a fall from higher than the person's own height, diving into shallow water.
- People over 65 after a fall.
- Neck or back pain, tingling, weakness or numbness in arms or legs.

## What to do if you suspect a spinal injury
1. Call the emergency number.
2. Do not move the person unless there is immediate danger (fire, traffic, water).
3. Ask them not to move their head. Hold their head with your hands, one on each side, so it stays still until help arrives.
4. Do not apply collars or tie the head: the guidelines do not recommend this for untrained people.
5. If they vomit or can't breathe well and you have to turn them, roll them as one unit (head, neck and body together), with help if possible.

## Head injury: concussion
After a blow, suspect a concussion if there is headache, dizziness, confusion, vomiting, blurred vision, unusual sleepiness, memory loss of what happened, or changes in behavior.
- Stop the activity at once (including sport) and have a doctor see them.
- Do not leave them alone for the first hours.

## Call the emergency number at once if...
- They lose consciousness even briefly, or are confused or drowsy.
- They have seizures, vomit repeatedly, or one pupil is bigger than the other.
- Blood or clear fluid comes from the nose or ears.
- They have trouble breathing or speaking, or weakness on one side of the body.
- They take blood thinners.

## Important
Based on the Canadian Red Cross, AHA and American Red Cross guidelines. Call your emergency number.
