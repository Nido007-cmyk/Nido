## Prepare
- Flashlights and batteries (better than candles), a battery radio, charged power banks.
- Water and food that need no cooking or refrigeration.
- Some cash: card payments and ATMs may not work.
- If someone depends on powered medical equipment (oxygen, ventilator, a fridge for insulin), have a backup plan and tell your power company and emergency services.
- Fill your car's tank if an outage is announced.

## During
- Unplug electronics to protect them when power returns. Leave one lamp on so you know when it's back.
- Keep the fridge and freezer closed (see the food guide).
- Use flashlights instead of candles. If you use candles, never leave them unattended and keep them away from curtains.
- **Generators**: always outdoors, more than 6 meters (20 feet) from doors and windows. Never inside the home, garage or a closed porch (carbon monoxide).
- Never heat the home with a gas oven, braziers or barbecues.
- In extreme heat or cold, go to a place with heating or cooling (shelters, public centers) if your home becomes dangerous.
- If traffic lights are out, treat intersections as an all-way stop.
- Avoid elevators during intermittent outages.
- Check on older or sick neighbors.

## Insulin and medicines that need to be cold
Insulin in use usually keeps for a few weeks at room temperature (not hot); check the leaflet. Keep it away from heat and direct light. Ask a pharmacist about other medicines.

## When power comes back
Wait a few minutes before plugging appliances in, one by one. Check food before eating it.
