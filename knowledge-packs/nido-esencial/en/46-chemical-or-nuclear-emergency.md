If there is a chemical or radioactive release nearby, the basic rule is: **get inside, stay inside, stay tuned**.

## Get inside
- Go into the nearest sturdy building, ideally brick or concrete.
- Inside, keep away from doors and windows: for radiation, go to the center of the building or the basement; for many chemicals, which are heavier than air, a higher floor is better.
- If you were outside during the release: take off your outer layer of clothing (it removes much of the contamination), put it in a bag, and shower with soap and water (no conditioner). If there is no shower, wash your hands, face and hair.

## Stay inside
- Close doors and windows, turn off fans, heating and air conditioning that draw in outside air.
- In a radiological emergency, stay inside for at least 24 hours unless told otherwise.
- Bring pets inside too.

## Stay tuned
- Listen to the radio or official sources and follow their instructions (evacuate or stay).
- **Potassium iodide**: it only protects the thyroid against radioactive iodine and is taken only if health authorities say so, at the dose they give.

## Chemical release outdoors
- Move upwind and to higher ground.
- Cover your mouth and nose with a cloth.
- If someone has chemicals on their skin or in their eyes, see the poisoning guide.

## Important
Based on recommendations from Ready.gov (FEMA), the CDC, the IAEA and the WHO.
