Cooling a burn with water early reduces damage and pain. It is the most important step.

## Steps
1. Move the person away from the heat source. If clothing is on fire: stop, drop and roll (or smother it with a blanket).
2. Cool the burn with cool (not ice-cold) running water for at least 10 minutes, the sooner the better. It still helps if a few minutes have passed.
3. While cooling, remove rings, watches and tight clothing from the area before it swells. Do not pull off clothing stuck to the skin.
4. Then cover with a non-stick dressing or clean kitchen plastic wrap (loosely).
5. Common painkillers can help with pain, if the person can take them.

## What NOT to do
- Do not use ice or ice water.
- Do not put toothpaste, oil, butter, egg or home remedies on serious burns.
- Do not pop blisters.
- In small children or large burns, do not cool so much that the person shivers: risk of hypothermia. Keep the rest of the body warm.

## Go to the emergency department or call the emergency number if...
- The burn is bigger than the palm of the person's hand.
- It affects the face, hands, feet, genitals or joints.
- The skin is white, black or leathery, or it doesn't hurt (deep burn).
- It is electrical or chemical.
- Smoke was inhaled: cough, hoarse voice, soot in the nose or mouth, trouble breathing.
- It is a baby, a small child or an older person.

## Chemical burns
Remove contaminated clothing (with gloves) and rinse with plenty of running water for a long time. For the eyes, rinse without stopping for at least 15 minutes and get help.

## Small burns
After cooling, a minor burn that won't be seen by a doctor can be treated with aloe vera, honey or petroleum jelly under a non-stick dressing.

## Important
Based on Red Cross and AHA guidelines. Call your emergency number for serious burns.
