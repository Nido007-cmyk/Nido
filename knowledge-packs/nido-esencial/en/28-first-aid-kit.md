Keep a kit at home, another in the car and a small one in your emergency bag. Check it every 6 months and replace expired items.

## Dressing supplies
- Disposable gloves (several pairs).
- Sterile gauze in various sizes and adhesive bandages.
- Elastic bandages and a triangular bandage (works as a sling).
- Adhesive tape.
- Large trauma dressings for heavy bleeding.
- A commercial tourniquet, if you know how to use it.
- Single-use saline vials for rinsing wounds and eyes.
- Antiseptic (for example chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine).
- Scissors, tweezers, safety pins.
- Foil (space) blanket.
- Instant cold packs.
- CPR face shield (barrier).

## Basic medicines
- For pain and fever: paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen.
- Aspirin (adults only, for a possible heart attack).
- Antihistamine for allergies.
- Oral rehydration salts sachets.
- Cream for minor burns or aloe gel.
- Your and your family's regular medicines for at least a week, with prescriptions.
- Adrenaline auto-injector if someone has severe allergies.

## Other
- Thermometer.
- Flashlight with spare batteries.
- Masks.
- Hand sanitizer.
- A list of each family member's allergies, conditions and medicines, and emergency phone numbers.
- A first aid guide (like this one).

## Tips
- Keep it in a cool, dry place everyone knows, out of children's reach.
- The most important part of the kit is knowing how to use it: take a first aid course.
