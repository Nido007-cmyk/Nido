Most small wounds heal well at home if they are cleaned and protected. The main goal is to prevent infection.

## Steps
1. Wash your hands.
2. If it bleeds, press with gauze or a clean cloth for a few minutes until it stops.
3. Clean the wound with plenty of drinkable water (tap water is ideal: the stream washes the dirt away). Rinse out soil or grit with the water.
4. You can apply antibiotic ointment; if you have none, an antiseptic.
5. Cover with a clean dressing or adhesive bandage. Change it every day or if it gets wet or dirty.

## See a doctor if...
- The wound is deep or gaping (the edges don't come together), is longer than 1–2 cm on the face, or still bleeds after 10 minutes of pressure.
- It was an animal or human bite, or something dirty or rusty caused it.
- Something is stuck in it (do not pull it out).
- Signs of infection appear: spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, more pain or fever.
- Your tetanus vaccine is not up to date (usually a booster every 10 years; after a dirty wound, if more than 5 years have passed).

## Blisters
- Do not pop them: they protect against infection. Cover them with a dressing.
- If one breaks, leave the skin on top, clean it and cover it.

## Important
Based on Red Cross and AHA guidelines. For serious wounds, follow the severe bleeding guide and call your emergency number.
