## If you get lost
1. **Stop.** Don't keep walking: the more you move, the bigger the search area.
2. Try calling the emergency number even without coverage from your provider: emergency calls can use other networks. If it doesn't connect, send a text message (it goes through with less signal).
3. Save battery: airplane mode between attempts, minimum brightness. Climb to a high point to look for signal.
4. Stay in an open, visible place near where you got lost, make a shelter and prepare signals.
5. Children should learn: if you get lost, **hug a tree** and stay there, and answer when you hear someone calling you.

## Distress signals
- **Three of anything** means distress: 3 whistle blasts, 3 flashes, 3 fires in a triangle, 3 piles of stones.
- **Whistle**: carries much farther than your voice and is less tiring. Mountain distress signal: 6 blasts in a minute, a minute's pause, and repeat. The reply is 3 per minute.
- **SOS** in Morse code: · · · — — — · · · (3 short, 3 long, 3 short), with a flashlight, whistle or knocking.
- **Mirror or shiny surface**: reflect the sun toward aircraft or distant people. Make a V with two fingers in front of you and aim the reflection between them.
- **Fire and smoke**: by day, smoke (add green leaves or damp branches for thick white smoke); at night, flames.
- **Colors**: spread brightly colored clothes or tarps in an open area.

## Ground signals for aircraft
Make them large (at least 3 meters / 10 feet per letter), with good contrast: stones, branches, clothes or trenches in snow.
- **V**: we need assistance.
- **X**: we need medical assistance.
- **SOS**: distress.
- **→** (arrow): we have gone this way.
- If an aircraft sees you, it may rock its wings to show it.

## Arm signals to a helicopter
- **Both arms raised (Y shape)**: yes, we need help.
- **One arm up and the other down**: no, we don't need help.
