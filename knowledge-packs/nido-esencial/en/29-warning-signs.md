Call the emergency number or go to the emergency department (ER) at once if you see any of these signs, in an adult or a child.

## In anyone
- Not responding, very confused or drowsy.
- Severe trouble breathing, blue lips or face.
- Strong chest pain or pressure.
- Signs of stroke: drooping face, weak arm, strange speech.
- Bleeding that won't stop.
- Seizures.
- Allergic reaction with trouble breathing or swelling of the face.
- Sudden, very severe headache, "the worst of their life".
- Very severe stomach pain, especially with a hard belly.
- Vomiting blood, or black tar-like stools.
- Large burns, or burns on the face, hands or genitals.
- Thoughts of ending their life.

## In children and babies (WHO danger signs)
- Cannot drink or breastfeed.
- Vomits everything.
- Has had a seizure.
- Very drowsy, floppy or hard to wake.
- Breathes very fast, the ribs sink in with each breath, or noisy breathing at rest.
- Fever in a baby under 3 months.
- Spots on the skin that don't fade when pressed.
- Dehydration: dry diapers, sunken eyes, crying without tears.

## In pregnancy
- Vaginal bleeding.
- Severe headache with blurred vision, or sudden swelling of the face and hands.
- Seizures.
- High fever.
- Severe stomach pain.
- The baby moves much less or stops moving.
- Leaking fluid before it is time.

## When you call
Say where you are, what is happening, how many people and their condition. Don't hang up until you are told to.

## Important
Based on WHO guidance (IMCI, ETAT), the AHA, the Canadian Red Cross and the NHS.
