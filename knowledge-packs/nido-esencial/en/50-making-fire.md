Fire gives warmth, light, lets you boil water, cook and signal. But it also causes wildfires: use it only when necessary and safe, and never during bans or in forbidden areas unless a life depends on it.

## Location
- Clear a circle about 3 meters (10 feet) across of dry leaves and grass. Away from trees, tents and your shelter.
- If you can, ring it with stones (not river stones: they can burst with heat) or dig a small pit.
- Sheltered from the wind.

## Three materials (prepare them BEFORE lighting)
1. **Tinder**: very fine, dry material that catches a spark: dry grass, thin bark, pine cones, lint, cotton with petroleum jelly, paper.
2. **Kindling**: dry sticks from the thickness of a match to a pencil. They should snap when broken.
3. **Fuel wood**: thicker sticks, from finger to wrist thickness and up.
Gather twice as much as you think you need. Dead wood that isn't touching the ground is usually drier.

## Lighting
- Put the tinder in the center and the kindling over it in a teepee shape, leaving a gap to light it from the windward side.
- Light the tinder from below and blow gently. Add kindling little by little, then thicker wood.
- Ways to light it: a lighter, matches (keep them in a waterproof bag), a ferrocerium rod (works when wet), a magnifying glass or the polished bottom of a can in sunlight, a 9-volt battery rubbed on steel wool.

## Safety
- Never leave a fire unattended.
- Never light a fire, brazier or barbecue inside a tent, closed cave, car or a house without a chimney: carbon monoxide kills without warning.
- To put it out: pour water, stir the embers and pour water again until everything is cold to the touch.

## Important
General information based on forestry services, Ready.gov (FEMA) and the NFPA. Respect fire bans.
