In a stroke, part of the brain loses its blood supply. Treatment works much better in the first hours: recognize it fast.

## Recognize it with FAST
- **Face**: ask them to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
- **Arms**: ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down or can't be lifted?
- **Speech**: ask them to repeat a simple sentence. Is speech slurred or strange, or do they not understand?
- **Time**: if you see any of these signs, call the emergency number at once.
Other sudden signs: loss of vision, severe dizziness with loss of balance, a sudden very severe headache.

## What to do
1. Call the emergency number and say you suspect a stroke.
2. Check the time: note when symptoms started, or the last time the person was seen well. This is key for treatment.
3. Let them sit or lie in a comfortable position. If they vomit or are drowsy, on their side.
4. Do not give food, drink or medicines (not even aspirin): they may choke, and some strokes are bleeds.
5. If they have diabetes and you have a glucose meter, you can check their sugar without delaying the call.

## Symptoms that go away on their own
Even if symptoms last only minutes and disappear, it is a serious warning (transient ischemic attack): a doctor must see them the same day.

## Important
Based on the AHA, American Red Cross and Canadian Red Cross guidelines. Call your emergency number.
