title: CPR for children and babies

In children, cardiac arrest is usually caused by lack of air (drowning, choking, suffocation). That is why rescue breaths matter more for children and babies than for adults. Even so, if you cannot give breaths, do compressions: it is always better than nothing.

## Child (1 year to puberty)
1. If the child does not respond and is not breathing normally, have someone call the emergency number and bring an AED. If you are alone and nobody saw the collapse, do about 2 minutes of CPR and then call.
2. Compressions with the heel of one hand (or two, for a big child) in the center of the chest.
3. Depth: one third of the chest, about 5 cm (2 inches). Rate: 100 to 120 per minute.
4. If you know how: 30 compressions and 2 breaths (head back, chin up, blow for 1 second until the chest rises).

## Baby (under 1 year)
1. Check by tapping the sole of the foot and talking to the baby. If there is no response and no normal breathing, start.
2. Lay the baby on their back on something firm.
3. Compressions in the center of the chest, just below the nipple line. The 2025 guidelines say to use the heel of one hand; you can also use two thumbs with your hands wrapped around the chest.
4. Depth: one third of the chest, about 4 cm (1.5 inches). Rate: 100 to 120 per minute.
5. Breaths: cover the baby's mouth and nose together with your mouth and blow gently, just enough to see the chest rise. Head in a neutral position (do not tilt it far back).
6. 30 compressions and 2 breaths if you are alone.

## AED for children
If there is a defibrillator, use it. If it has child pads or a child mode, use them for children under 8; if not, use the adult pads (one on the chest and one on the back if they don't fit apart).

## Important
Based on the AHA 2025 guidelines and the Red Cross. If you look after children, take a pediatric first aid course. Always call your emergency number.
