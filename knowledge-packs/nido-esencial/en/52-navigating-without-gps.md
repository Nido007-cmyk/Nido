## First: move or stay?
If you are lost and someone knows where you were going, the best choice is usually to **stay put** in a visible place: you'll be found sooner. Move only if you are in danger or know for sure where to go.

## The sun
- It rises roughly in the **east** and sets roughly in the **west** (exactly only at the equinoxes; it varies with the season).
- At midday, in the northern hemisphere the sun is to the **south**; in the southern hemisphere, to the **north**.
- **Shadow-stick method**: push a straight stick into the ground. Mark the tip of the shadow with a stone. Wait 15 to 20 minutes and mark it again. The line from the first mark to the second points roughly **west to east**.

## The stars
- **Northern hemisphere**: find the Big Dipper (the "plough"). Extend the distance between the two outer stars of the dipper's bowl about 5 times: you'll reach the **North Star (Polaris)**, which marks **north**.
- **Southern hemisphere**: the **Southern Cross**. Extend its long axis about 4.5 times downwards: that point is above **south**.

## Signs in the landscape (unreliable on their own)
- Moss and branch growth vary a lot: don't rely on them alone.
- Following a river downstream often leads to settlements, but it can lead to waterfalls and gorges: be careful.
- Paths, power lines and fences usually lead somewhere inhabited.

## Walking in a straight line
Pick a distant point in the direction you want (a tree, a mountain), walk to it and pick another. Without landmarks, people walk in circles.

## Before it happens
- Download offline maps on your phone and carry a paper map and compass.
- Study the map before you go: rivers, roads, nearby towns.
