Someone who is drowning almost never shouts or waves: they slip under silently within seconds.

## Rescue without putting yourself in danger
Many rescuers die trying to save others. Follow this order:
1. **Shout for help**: have someone call the emergency number.
2. **Reach** from land: hold out a stick, towel, branch or rope. Lie down on the ground so you don't fall in.
3. **Throw** something that floats: a life ring, an empty cooler, a large closed bottle, a ball.
4. **Go into the water only if** you are trained, and if possible take something that floats for them to hold.
In river currents or surf, do not go in. On broken ice, lie flat to spread your weight and throw a rope.

## Once out of the water
1. If they do not respond and are not breathing normally, call the emergency number and start CPR. For drowning, start with 5 rescue breaths if you know how, then 30 compressions and 2 breaths.
2. Do not waste time trying to get water out of the lungs, and don't press on the stomach.
3. If they vomit, turn them on their side and clear the mouth.
4. If they are breathing, put them on their side, dry them and keep them warm.
5. Even if they seem fine, a doctor should see them: breathing problems can appear hours later.

## Prevention
- Constant supervision of children near water, within arm's reach. A child can drown in a few centimeters of water (buckets, bathtubs).
- Life jackets on boats and for anyone who can't swim well.
- Don't swim after drinking alcohol or in unknown waters. Don't dive head first where you can't see the bottom.
- If a current pulls you, don't fight it: swim parallel to the shore until you are out of it, or float and call for help.
