## Recovery position
For a person who does not respond but is breathing normally (and no suspected spinal injury):
1. Kneel beside them. Bend the arm nearest to you upwards.
2. Bring their other hand to the cheek on your side and hold it there.
3. Bend the far knee and pull it towards you to roll them onto their side.
4. Tilt the head slightly back so air can pass and the mouth faces down (if they vomit, it drains out).
5. Call the emergency number and keep checking that they are breathing. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR.

## Shock
Shock is when not enough blood reaches the organs: from bleeding, burns, a severe allergy, a heart attack or dehydration. Signs: pale, cold, sweaty skin, fast pulse, fast breathing, dizziness, thirst, confusion.
1. Call the emergency number and treat the cause (for example, stop the bleeding).
2. Lay them on their back and keep them warm (also underneath if they are on cold ground).
3. If it makes them feel better, you can raise their legs a little for a few minutes.
4. If they have trouble breathing, let them half-sit. If you suspect a heart attack, reclined with the back somewhat raised.
5. Do not give food or drink.

## Fainting
- If someone feels they are about to faint (dizziness, blurred vision, cold sweat): have them sit or lie down at once. Crossing the legs and squeezing them, or clenching the fists hard, may help prevent it.
- If they faint: lay them on their back, check they are breathing and move them away from danger. If they don't come round within a minute, call the emergency number.
- Any faint with loss of consciousness should be checked by a doctor, especially with chest pain, palpitations, during exercise, or in an older person.

## Important
Based on Red Cross and AHA guidelines. Call your emergency number.
