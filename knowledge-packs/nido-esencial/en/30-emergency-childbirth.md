Most births go well on their own. If the baby arrives before help does, your role is to support, keep things clean and keep them warm.

## Before the baby is born
1. Call the emergency number and follow their instructions.
2. Wash your hands and put on gloves if you have them.
3. Prepare a clean place: clean sheets or towels, something to wrap the baby in.
4. Let the mother take the most comfortable position (half-sitting or on her side).
5. Do not pull on the baby or try to delay the birth.

## When the baby comes out
1. Support the head gently as it comes out; the body comes with the next contraction.
2. If the cord is around the neck, gently slip it over the head.
3. Dry the baby at once with a clean towel, especially the head, and rub the back.
4. Place the baby skin to skin on the mother's chest and cover both. Cover the baby's head.
5. The baby should cry or breathe within the first minute. If not breathing after drying and rubbing, start infant CPR.
6. Encourage breastfeeding within the first hour.

## The cord and the placenta
- There is no need to cut the cord. If help will be a long time and you have clean materials: wait at least 1 to 3 minutes, tie the cord with clean string in two places (about 10 and 15 cm from the baby) and cut between them with boiled scissors or blade.
- The placenta comes out on its own 5 to 30 minutes later. Do not pull on the cord. Keep it in a bag for a professional to check.
- Afterwards, gently massaging the belly below the navel helps the womb contract and bleed less.

## Get urgent help if...
- A foot, the buttocks or the cord comes out first.
- The mother bleeds heavily (more than soaks two pads or towels).
- The baby isn't breathing well, is blue or floppy.
- The mother has a seizure or faints.

## Important
Based on WHO, Canadian Red Cross and American Red Cross recommendations. Mother and baby should be checked by a professional as soon as possible.
