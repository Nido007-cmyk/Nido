Anaphylaxis is a severe allergic reaction that can kill within minutes. It usually appears soon after a food (nuts, shellfish, egg, milk), a bee or wasp sting, or a medicine.

## Signs
- Trouble breathing, wheezing, hoarse voice, a feeling that the throat is closing.
- Swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat.
- Hives or itching all over, flushing.
- Dizziness, fainting, pale and cold skin (shock), vomiting or stomach pain.

## What to do
1. Call the emergency number at once.
2. If the person has an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector, such as an EpiPen, help them use it on the outer thigh (it can go through clothing). Follow the device instructions and hold it in place for the time it says.
3. If there is no improvement after 5–10 minutes, help has not arrived yet and a second auto-injector is available, a second dose can be given.
4. Position: if breathing is hard, sitting up. If dizzy or pale, lying down with the legs slightly raised. Do not stand them up or make them walk.
5. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally, start CPR.
6. Even if they get better, they must go to hospital: the reaction can come back hours later.

## Bee stings
Remove the stinger as soon as possible: scrape it gently with something flat, like a bank card or the blunt side of a knife. Do not pinch it or use tweezers: they can squeeze the venom sac and make the reaction worse. Wash with soap and water and apply cold.

## Important
Based on the AHA, American Red Cross and Canadian Red Cross guidelines. If you know you have severe allergies, always carry your auto-injector and teach your family how to use it. Call your emergency number.
