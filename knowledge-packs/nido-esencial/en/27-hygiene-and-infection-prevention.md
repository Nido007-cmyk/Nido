Washing your hands with soap is one of the cheapest and most effective ways to avoid illnesses such as diarrhea and respiratory infections.

## How to wash your hands
1. Wet your hands with clean water.
2. Apply soap and rub for at least 20 seconds: palms, backs, between the fingers, nails and thumbs.
3. Rinse and dry with a clean towel or in the air.
If there is no soap and water, use hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol. If there is nothing, ash and water is better than water alone.

## When to wash
- Before cooking, eating or feeding someone.
- After using the toilet, changing diapers or cleaning up a sick person.
- After coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose.
- Before and after treating a wound.
- After touching animals or garbage.

## If someone at home is sick
- If it is a respiratory infection: they should wear a mask near others; ventilate the home; cough into the elbow.
- Use separate towels, cups and cutlery.
- Wash clothes and bedding with hot water and soap.
- Clean frequently touched surfaces.
- Disinfecting with household bleach: for surfaces, generally about 1 part bleach to 50 to 100 parts water; always following the label and with ventilation. Never mix bleach with ammonia or with vinegar or other acids: it produces toxic gases.

## Safe food (WHO Five Keys)
1. Keep clean.
2. Separate raw and cooked food.
3. Cook thoroughly, especially meat, eggs and fish.
4. Keep food at safe temperatures: don't leave cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
5. Use safe water and raw materials.

## Vaccines
Vaccines protect you and those around you. Keep a copy (photo) of your vaccination record.
