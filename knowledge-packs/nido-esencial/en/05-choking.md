---
title: Choking in adults, children and babies
category: First aid
lang: en
sources: AHA Guidelines 2025 (choking); American Red Cross; IFRC 2020
---
If something blocks the throat, the person cannot breathe. Watch for this: if they cough forcefully, they are getting air; if they cannot speak, cough or breathe, or clutch their throat, it is serious.

## Mild choking (coughing forcefully)
- Encourage them to keep coughing. Do not hit their back or give water. Stay with them and watch.

## Severe, in adults and children over 1 year
The 2025 guidelines recommend alternating back blows and abdominal thrusts:
1. Stand beside and slightly behind them. Lean the person forward, supporting their chest.
2. Give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. If it doesn't come out, give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver): stand behind them, wrap your arms around the waist, put your fist (thumb side) above the navel and below the breastbone, grab it with the other hand and pull sharply in and up.
4. Alternate 5 and 5 until the object comes out or they stop responding.
- Pregnant or very large people: give thrusts on the chest (lower half of the breastbone), not the abdomen.
- If you are alone and choking: call the emergency number if you can, and push your abdomen against the back of a chair or the edge of a table.

## Severe, in babies (under 1 year)
1. Lay the baby face down along your forearm, head lower than the body, supporting the jaw (without covering the mouth).
2. Give 5 back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. Turn the baby face up on your other forearm, head lower, and give 5 chest thrusts (center, just below the nipples). The 2025 guidelines say to use the heel of one hand.
4. Alternate 5 and 5. Never give abdominal thrusts to a baby.
5. Look in the mouth: if you see the object and it is easy, take it out. Never sweep blindly with a finger.

## If they stop responding
- Call (or have someone call) the emergency number, lay them on the floor and start CPR. Each time you open the airway to give breaths, look for the object.

## Afterwards
Even if it was cleared, if abdominal thrusts were used, or coughing, pain or trouble breathing continue, a doctor should see them.

## Important
Based on the AHA 2025 guidelines and the Red Cross. Practice these maneuvers in a course: they are not practiced with force on healthy people.
