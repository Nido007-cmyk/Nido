A healthy person can go weeks without food, but not without water. Don't eat if you have very little water: digestion uses up fluid.

## Supplies at home
Foods that need no refrigeration and little cooking: canned food (with a manual can opener), canned beans, tuna, nuts, bars, crackers, peanut butter, shelf-stable or powdered milk, cereals, dried fruit. Keep babies, allergies and special diets in mind.

## Without power: the fridge
- Keep the doors closed. A fridge keeps cold for about 4 hours; a full freezer, about 48 hours (24 if half full).
- Eat what's in the fridge first, then the freezer, and canned food last.
- Throw away meat, fish, milk, eggs and cooked food that have been above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 2 hours. **When in doubt, throw it out.**
- Don't taste food to check if it's OK.
- Bulging, rusty or seam-dented cans: throw them away.

## In the wild
- **Do not eat plants or mushrooms you don't know with complete certainty.** Many are deadly and look like edible ones. Poisoning in that situation is far worse than hunger.
- Safer sources if you can identify them: fish, well-cooked insects (grasshoppers, larvae; avoid brightly colored, hairy or strong-smelling ones), fruits and plants you know well.
- Cook all meat, fish and insects thoroughly.
- It's better to save energy (rest, stay warm) than spend it looking for food.
