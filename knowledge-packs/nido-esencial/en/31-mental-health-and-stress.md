Emergencies, uncertainty and loss affect the mind as much as the body. After an earthquake, a flood or another disaster it is common to feel anxious, be unable to sleep, or keep reliving what happened. Feeling fear, sadness or anger is a normal reaction to something abnormal.

## Simple stress techniques (WHO)
- **Grounding**: when an emotion overwhelms you, feel your feet on the floor, straighten your back, breathe slowly and notice 5 things you see and 5 you hear.
- **Slow breathing**: breathe in counting to 4, out counting to 6. Repeat for a few minutes.
- **Engaging with what matters**: do small things that are meaningful to you, even if you feel bad: helping someone, caring for someone, your beliefs.
- **Routines**: regular times for sleep, meals and some activity each day.
- **Connect**: talk to people you trust. Isolating yourself makes things worse.
- Limit exposure to distressing news. Avoid alcohol and other substances to "cope".

## Panic attack
- A panic attack is a sudden wave of intense fear with strong physical reactions: racing heart, tight chest, shortness of breath, dizziness, or a feeling of losing control. It is very frightening, but it passes.
- Stay with the person calmly: sit with them, speak slowly and in a calm tone, without arguing about what they feel or telling them it is nothing.
- Help them breathe slowly: breathe in counting to 4 and out counting to 6, at a pace they can manage.
- Call the emergency number if the symptoms do not ease, if it is the first time it happens to them, if there is chest pain or fainting, or if you are not sure it is a panic attack: its symptoms resemble those of heart or breathing problems.

## Children
Explain what is happening in simple words and tell the truth. Keep routines, play with them, let them express what they feel (drawings, games). It is normal for them to behave as if younger for a while.

## Grief
There is no right way to live through a loss. Rituals, talking about the person and support from others help.
- Listen without pressing: let the person talk about their loss if they want to, without forcing it, and respect their silence too.
- Respect each person's process: grief has no right way and no fixed timeline; rituals and saying goodbye help many people.
- Connect the person with their social support: family, friends, their community or their beliefs, and keep the support going over time, not only in the first days.

## Ask for professional help if...
- Sadness, fear or sleeplessness last more than 2 weeks or stop you from living your life.
- You use alcohol or other drugs to get through it.
- You have thoughts of hurting yourself or that life is not worth living.

## If someone talks about ending their life
- Take it seriously. Ask directly; talking about it doesn't put the idea in their head, it brings relief.
- Listen without judging. Don't leave them alone if there is immediate risk.
- Remove things they could use to hurt themselves.
- Get help: the emergency number or a crisis line in your country.
