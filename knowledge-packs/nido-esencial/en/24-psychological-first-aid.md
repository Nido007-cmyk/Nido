After an accident, a disaster or bad news, people may be in shock, frightened, confused or frozen. You don't need to be a psychologist to help. The WHO sums it up in three words: **look, listen and link**.

## Look
- Is the place safe? Who needs urgent basic help (injured people, children on their own, older people)?
- Who is very distressed, disoriented or not reacting?

## Listen
- Approach calmly, introduce yourself and ask what they need.
- Listen without pressure. Don't force anyone to tell what happened.
- Help them calm down: speak slowly, breathe with them, help them feel their feet on the ground and look around them.
- Helpful phrases: "I'm here with you", "It's normal to feel this way", "Let's see together what you need right now".
- Avoid: "Don't cry", "It could have been worse", "I know how you feel", judging, or promising things you don't know.

## Link
- Help meet basic needs: water, food, warmth, a quiet place, charging a phone.
- Help them contact family or loved ones.
- Give clear, true information about what is happening and where to get help.
- Keep children together with their caregivers.

## Normal reactions afterwards
For days or weeks it is normal to feel fear, have nightmares, sleep badly, be irritable, cry or feel guilty. It usually improves with time, rest, routine and support from others. Seek professional help if it doesn't improve after a few weeks or stops normal life.

## Immediate risk
If someone talks about ending their life or cannot look after themselves, don't leave them alone and get urgent help from the emergency number or a crisis line.

## Look after yourself too
Helping is tiring. Rest, eat, talk to someone you trust.
