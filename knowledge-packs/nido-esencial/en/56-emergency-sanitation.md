When there is no running water or the sewer fails, badly managed feces are one of the biggest causes of illness after a disaster.

## Emergency toilet at home
- If there is no water to flush or the sewer may be damaged, don't flush.
- Use a bucket with a lid (or the toilet itself) lined with two strong garbage bags.
- After each use, cover with a handful of drying material: sawdust, cat litter, dry soil, ash or lime. It reduces smell and flies.
- Tie the bag well when half full and keep it in a covered container, away from the house and water, until it can be collected.
- Keep urine separate if possible: it can be poured on soil away from water sources.

## Outdoor latrine
- At least 30 meters (100 feet) from any water source (well, river) and downhill from it.
- A narrow trench 30 to 50 cm (1 to 1.5 feet) deep. Cover with soil after each use.
- Give it privacy, especially for women and girls, and light at night if possible.

## Hygiene
- Wash your hands with soap and water (or ash) after using the toilet and before eating. Set up a handwashing point with a container with a tap or a hanging bottle.
- Keep garbage in closed bags and covered containers, away from food and water areas.
- Get rid of standing water so mosquitoes don't breed.

## Menstrual hygiene
Disposable or reusable pads (washed with soap and dried in the sun), bags to dispose of them, water and privacy.
