Fever (38 °C / 100.4 °F or higher) is the body's defense against infection. It is almost always mild, but you need to know when it is serious.

## What to do
- Drink plenty of fluids: water, broth, oral rehydration solution. Breastfeed babies more often.
- Light clothing, a cool room. Don't over-wrap, and don't bathe in cold water or alcohol.
- If they feel unwell, paracetamol (acetaminophen) or ibuprofen can be used at the dose on the label for age and weight. Do not give aspirin to anyone under 16.
- Rest. Wash hands often so as not to spread it.

## Get urgent medical care if...
- It is a baby under 3 months with 38 °C (100.4 °F) or more.
- They have trouble breathing, breathe very fast, or the skin sinks in between the ribs with each breath.
- They are very floppy, not reacting well, confused or very irritable.
- They have a rash that doesn't fade when you press a glass on it.
- Stiff neck, very severe headache, or strong discomfort with light.
- They have a seizure, cannot drink, or vomit everything.
- Signs of dehydration: no urine, very dry mouth, sunken eyes, crying without tears.
- It lasts more than 3 days, or comes back after going away.
- They have come back from an area with malaria or dengue.

## Areas with malaria or dengue
Any fever in a malaria area needs a test as soon as possible. In dengue, avoid ibuprofen and aspirin (they raise the risk of bleeding): use paracetamol and get advice.
