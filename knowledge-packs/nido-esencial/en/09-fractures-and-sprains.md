Without an X-ray you cannot know whether a bone is broken. So after an injury with strong pain, swelling, deformity or inability to move it, treat it as a fracture.

## Steps
1. Do not move the injured part. Let the person support it in the most comfortable position.
2. Immobilize it in the position you found it: do not try to straighten a bent bone.
3. To immobilize, you can use an improvised splint (cardboard, rolled-up magazine, board) padded with clothing, secured above and below the injury. An arm can be supported with a scarf as a sling.
4. Apply cold wrapped in cloth (never ice directly on the skin) for no more than 20 minutes.
5. Check that the fingers or toes stay warm and pink; if they turn pale, cold or blue, loosen the support.
6. Watch for signs of shock (pale, cold skin, sweating, dizziness), especially with a thigh or pelvis injury: these can bleed a lot internally.

## Open fracture (bone showing or a wound over it)
Cover the wound with a clean dressing, control bleeding by pressing around it (not on the bone) and immobilize. Call the emergency number.

## Sprains and twists
- Rest, cold wrapped in cloth for up to 20 minutes at a time, a bandage that is not tight, and the limb raised if comfortable.
- See a doctor if you cannot put weight on the foot or move the joint, if there is visible deformity, or if it doesn't improve in a few days.

## Call the emergency number if...
- It is a fracture of the thigh, pelvis, neck or back, or the bone is showing.
- There is heavy bleeding, the limb is cold and pale, or the person shows signs of shock.

## Important
Based on the Canadian Red Cross and IFRC guidelines. Only trained personnel should straighten a fracture.
