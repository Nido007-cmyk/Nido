## Hypothermia
The body cools below normal. It can happen with cold, wind, rain or after falling into water, even when it is not freezing.
Signs: strong shivering, cold and pale skin, clumsiness, slow speech, confusion, sleepiness. In severe cases shivering stops: that is a worse sign, not a better one.

### What to do
1. Call the emergency number if they are confused, very drowsy or not responding.
2. Get them to a sheltered place. Move them gently.
3. Remove wet clothing and dry them.
4. Wrap their whole body in blankets, sleeping bags or dry clothes, including the head and neck. Insulate them from the ground.
5. If you have a foil (space) blanket, put it over the warm layers.
6. You can place warm water bottles wrapped in cloth on the chest, armpits and groin.
7. If they are awake and swallow well, give warm sweet drinks (no alcohol or caffeine).
8. Do not rub their arms or legs and do not put them in hot water.
9. If they don't respond and are not breathing normally, start CPR.

## Frostbite
Skin and tissue freeze, especially fingers, toes, ears, nose and cheeks.
Signs: white or grayish skin, hard or waxy, numb.
1. Move to a warm place and treat hypothermia first if present.
2. Remove rings and tight clothing.
3. Do not rub the area or hold it near a fire or heater.
4. If there is no risk of it freezing again, rewarm the area in warm water (37 to 40 °C, 99 to 104 °F; comfortable to hold your hand in) for 20 to 30 minutes. If it may refreeze, it is better not to thaw it yet.
5. Do not burst blisters. Cover with clean gauze and separate the fingers or toes.
6. Get medical care.
