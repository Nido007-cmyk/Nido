During a seizure the person goes stiff, jerks and does not respond. It usually lasts less than 2 or 3 minutes. Your goal is to keep them from getting hurt.

## During the seizure
1. Stay calm and note the time.
2. Move hard or dangerous objects away. Put something soft under their head.
3. Loosen clothing around the neck.
4. Do NOT hold them down or try to stop the movements.
5. Do NOT put anything in their mouth: they will not swallow their tongue.

## Afterwards
1. When the jerking stops, turn them on their side (recovery position) and check they are breathing.
2. Stay with them: it is normal to be confused or tired for a while. Talk calmly.
3. Give no food, drink or medicine until they are fully awake.

## Call the emergency number if...
- It is their first seizure or you don't know if they have epilepsy.
- It lasts more than 5 minutes, or another one starts without recovery in between.
- They are not back to normal within 5 to 10 minutes.
- They are injured, have trouble breathing, or have choked.
- It happened in water.
- They are pregnant, or a baby under 6 months.

## Fever seizures in children
Young children from 6 months to 5 years can have seizures with high fever. Do the same and have a doctor see them. Lowering the fever with medicine does not prevent these seizures.
