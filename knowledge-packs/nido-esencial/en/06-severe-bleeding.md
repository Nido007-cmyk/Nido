---
title: Severe bleeding: how to stop it
category: First aid
lang: en
sources: AHA and American Red Cross First Aid Guidelines 2024; Canadian Red Cross 2020; IFRC 2020
---
A person can bleed to death in a few minutes. Bleeding is severe if blood spurts or keeps flowing, soaks clothes or bandages, or if the person becomes dizzy, pale or confused. Act at once.

## Steps
1. Call (or have someone call) the emergency number.
2. Put on gloves or a plastic bag if you have them; if not, don't wait.
3. Direct pressure: press hard on the wound with gauze, a clean cloth or, if there is nothing else, your hand. Press with both hands and your body weight.
4. Do not remove the cloth if it soaks through: add more on top and keep pressing without stopping.
5. Keep the pressure until help arrives. The wound can bleed again if you let go.

## Tourniquet (arms and legs)
If bleeding from an arm or leg is life-threatening and pressure is not enough or you cannot keep it up:
- Use a commercial tourniquet if there is one. Put it 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 inches) above the wound (never over a joint) and tighten it until the bleeding stops. It hurts: that is normal.
- Note the time you put it on and tell emergency services. Do not loosen it.
- An improvised one (belt, wide cloth with a stick to twist) works worse; use it only if there is no other option.

## Packing the wound
If the wound is deep in the groin, armpit or neck (where a tourniquet won't fit), pack it with gauze or clean cloth pushed inwards, and keep firm pressure.

## What NOT to do
- Do not raise the limb or look for "pressure points" instead of pressing on the wound: these are not recommended.
- Do not pull out embedded objects: keep them from moving and press around them.

## While you wait
Keep the person warm and lying down (risk of shock). Talk to them and check that they respond and breathe.

## Important
Based on the AHA, American Red Cross and Canadian Red Cross guidelines. Free "Stop the Bleed" courses teach how to use a tourniquet. Call your emergency number.
