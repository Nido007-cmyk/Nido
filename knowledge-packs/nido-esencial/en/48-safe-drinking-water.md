Untreated water can carry diarrhea, cholera, typhoid and parasites. If in doubt, treat it.

## Where to find water in an emergency at home
- The water in the hot water tank (shut the inlet and drain it from the bottom).
- Ice cubes, the liquid in canned vegetables.
- The toilet tank (not the bowl), if it has no chemicals: treat it first.
- Don't use water from radiators, pools (except for washing) or floods.

## In the wild
- Running water is better than standing water. Collect rainwater on tarps or in clean containers.
- Look at the bottom of valleys, where vegetation is green, or dig on the outside of a bend in a dry riverbed.
- Morning dew can be collected with a cloth and wrung out.
- Do not drink seawater or urine: they dehydrate you more.

## How to make water safe
**1. First, clear it.** If it's cloudy, let it settle and strain it through a clean cloth or coffee filter.

**2. Choose a method:**
- **Boiling** (the safest): bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 meters / 6,500 feet). Let it cool covered.
- **Household bleach**, unscented (5–6% hypochlorite): 2 drops per liter of clear water (4 drops if cloudy or very cold). Stir and wait 30 minutes. It should smell slightly of chlorine; if not, repeat the dose and wait 15 more minutes. If your bleach has a different strength, follow the label.
- **Purification tablets**: follow the instructions.
- **Portable water filters**: remove bacteria and parasites; many don't remove viruses. Combine with chlorine if unsure.
- **Solar disinfection (SODIS)**: fill clear PET plastic bottles (up to 2 liters) with clear water, close them and lay them in direct sun for 6 hours (2 days if cloudy). Doesn't work with cloudy water.

## Keep in mind
- No home method removes chemicals, fuel or metals. If the water smells of gasoline or chemicals, don't use it.
- Store treated water in clean, covered containers and take it out with a clean ladle.
- Amount: you need at least 2 liters a day to drink, more in heat or with effort.
