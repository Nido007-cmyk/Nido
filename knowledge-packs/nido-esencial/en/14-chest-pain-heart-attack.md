In a heart attack, every minute counts. Do not wait to see if it passes.

## Signs
- Pain, pressure or tightness in the chest lasting more than a few minutes, or that comes and goes.
- Pain spreading to the arm (especially the left), jaw, neck, back or stomach.
- Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, dizziness.
- In women, older people and people with diabetes, signs can be vaguer: extreme tiredness, nausea, shortness of breath without clear pain.

## What to do
1. Call the emergency number at once.
2. Have the person stop any effort. Sit them half-reclined, in the most comfortable position.
3. If it is an adult who is awake, not allergic to aspirin, not bleeding, and whose doctor has not told them to avoid it: have them chew one adult aspirin (162 to 325 mg, or 2 to 4 low-dose 81 mg aspirins) and swallow it.
4. If they have prescribed angina medicine (nitroglycerin), help them take it.
5. Loosen clothing, reassure them and don't leave them alone.
6. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally, start CPR and ask for a defibrillator.

## Important
Based on the AHA, American Red Cross and Canadian Red Cross guidelines. Do not drive the person yourself if you can wait for an ambulance: emergency crews start treatment on the way.
