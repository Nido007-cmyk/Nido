A tsunami is a series of huge waves, usually caused by an undersea earthquake. They can arrive within minutes.

## Natural warnings: act without waiting for an alarm
- A strong or long earthquake (hard to stand, or lasting more than 20 seconds) while you are on the coast.
- The sea suddenly pulls back, exposing the seabed.
- A loud roar coming from the ocean.
If you notice any of these signs, leave now.

## What to do
1. Move away from the coast and go to high ground: ideally at least 30 meters (100 feet) above sea level or 3 kilometers (2 miles) inland. Follow evacuation route signs.
2. Go on foot if you can: cars cause traffic jams.
3. If you cannot get away, go to the upper floors of a strong concrete building, or a tall, sturdy tree as a last resort.
4. If you are on a boat, head out to sea, into deep water.
5. If the water sweeps you away, grab something that floats.

## After
- Don't return until the authorities say it is safe: several waves arrive, and the first is often not the biggest. The danger can last for hours.
- Stay away from rivers and streams that flow into the sea.
- Be careful with floodwater: it can carry debris and live power lines and be contaminated.

## Before
Find out whether you live, work or vacation in a tsunami zone, what the evacuation routes are, and how long it takes on foot to reach high ground.
