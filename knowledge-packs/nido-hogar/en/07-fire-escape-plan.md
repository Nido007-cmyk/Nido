The general family plan in NIDO Essentials covers any disaster. This plan is for fire only, and it is more demanding: you may have only **2 minutes** to get out, at night and in smoke. A plan that is never practiced is not a plan.

## Make the plan (one evening, with everyone)
1. **Draw a map of your home** showing all doors and windows.
2. **Find two ways out of every room.** The second way can be a window; for upper floors, an escape ladder kept in that room.
3. **Check that the exits work:** windows open (not stuck or painted shut), screens come out quickly and security bars can be released from inside. If a window will not open, fix it now.
4. **Keep escape paths clear:** no furniture or piles blocking doors, windows or exit hallways.
5. **Assign help:** decide which adult will help each young child (children under six cannot get out by themselves), older adults or people with disabilities, and pets. Assign a backup person too.
6. **Choose the meeting place:** one fixed, safe spot, a safe distance from the home and always the same: a specific tree, the mailbox, a neighbor's home. Everyone goes there and nowhere else.
7. **Write it down and keep it visible** (for example, on the refrigerator), including how to call the emergency services from outside.

## Practice it (at least twice a year)
1. Gather everyone and review the meeting place: when the alarm sounds, you get out and go there.
2. Test the smoke alarms with the children present, so they know the sound and do not hide when they hear it.
3. Run the drill: everyone in their rooms, sound the alarm and **time it**. The goal is everyone at the meeting place in **under 2 minutes**.
4. Practice **getting low**, moving under the smoke, and once **in the dark** or with eyes closed, feeling your way out.
5. Practice the second way out of a room, not always the door.
6. Vary the time of day (day and night) and the routes in each drill.
7. Afterwards, count heads at the meeting place and note what went wrong: a stiff window, a slow route, a child who did not hear the alarm. Fix it before the next drill.
Teach children two things about firefighters: there is no need to hide from them, and you **never go back inside** a burning home, not for people, pets or things.

## In a real fire, the plan is in charge
1. When the alarm sounds or you see smoke, follow the plan without stopping for belongings.
2. Before opening a door, feel it with the **back of your hand**: if it is hot, use your second way out.
3. Get low, close doors behind you and go **straight to the meeting place**.
4. Count everyone at the meeting place. If someone is missing, tell the firefighters when they arrive: **do not go in to search yourself**.
5. Call the emergency number from outside and stay at the meeting place until help arrives.
