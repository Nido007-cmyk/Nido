An electrical fire often starts with no flames at all: a damaged cord, an overloaded outlet or a failing appliance heats whatever is near it until it catches. The warning signs come before the fire; take them seriously.

## Warning signs (call a qualified electrician)
- Fuses blow or breakers trip often.
- A tingling feeling when you touch an electrical appliance.
- Wall outlets or switches that are warm or discolored.
- A burning or rubbery smell coming from an appliance.
- Lights that flicker or dim for no reason.
- Sparks from an outlet.
Stop using the affected appliance or outlet until a professional has checked it. All electrical work should be done by a qualified electrician.

## If an appliance sputters, smokes or smells odd
1. **Shut it off and unplug it right away**, if you can do so without touching anything burned, without being wet and without reaching near the sparks.
2. If you cannot unplug it safely, switch off that circuit at the breaker panel.
3. Do not use it again: have it repaired or replaced. An appliance that gives shocks, overheats, shorts out or gives off smoke or sparks is no longer trustworthy.
4. If there are flames, go to the next section.

## If there is a fire
1. **Do not use water** on plugged-in equipment or a breaker panel: water can conduct electricity. An extinguisher carrying the letter C, Class ABC or BC, is suitable for an electrical fire (see the extinguisher guide in this pack).
2. If the plug or panel is within reach and getting to it does not take you through fire or smoke, unplug the appliance or switch off that circuit.
3. If the fire is small and contained in a single appliance, you may try the extinguisher with your exit at your back.
4. If the fire touches wall wiring, the breaker panel or anything else, or it is growing: **get out, close the door and call the emergency number from outside.**

## When to cut the power to the whole house
Switch off the main breaker if there is smoke or a burning smell coming from the wiring, a fire involving cables or outlets, or wet appliances. But:
- **Never touch the panel if you are wet, standing in water or unsure it is safe.** In that case leave and ask for help: the power company can cut it off.
- **Do not touch switches or the panel if you suspect a gas leak**: a spark could ignite the gas (see the gas leak guide in this pack).

## Prevention at home
- Do not overload outlets or power strips; plug only one heat-producing appliance (toaster, space heater, iron) into each outlet.
- Major appliances (refrigerator, washer, dryer, stove) go straight into a wall outlet, never on an extension cord: the cord can overheat.
- Unplug small appliances when you are not using them.
- Replace worn or damaged cords and extension cords right away; do not run them under carpets or where they can be stepped on or pinched.
- Insert plugs fully; if a plug fits loosely, replace that outlet. Never force a 3-prong cord into a 2-slot outlet.
- Use light bulbs that match the wattage on the lamp or fixture, and keep clothes, curtains and paper away from lamps and light fixtures.
