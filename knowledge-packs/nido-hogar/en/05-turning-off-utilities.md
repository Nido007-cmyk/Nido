Knowing how to shut off your utilities keeps a breakdown from becoming a disaster: a broken pipe floods, damaged wiring catches fire, leaking gas explodes. Learn where each valve and switch is **today**, with no emergency on, and show everyone else in the household.

## Before: find them and prepare
1. Locate the main water valve, the gas valve next to the meter and the electrical panel.
2. Check that the valves turn (without fully closing them if you do not need to) and keep any tool they need nearby, for example a wrench by the gas and water valves.
3. Label what each breaker in the panel turns off.
4. Show all responsible household members how and when to turn each utility off.
In an apartment, ask your landlord or building manager where the meters and the building's main valves are.

## Water
**Where it usually is:** where the street supply pipe enters the house; it may also be in the garage, laundry room, basement or on an exterior wall. The buried box near the street belongs to the utility and may need a special tool.
**When to shut it off:** if a pipe is damaged or bursts, if you see a major leak, or after a disaster that damaged pipes.
**How:**
1. Turn the main valve until it is fully closed (for a screw-type valve, clockwise until it stops).
2. Open a low tap to check that the water has stopped.
You can turn the water back on yourself once the damage is repaired.

## Electricity
**Where it usually is:** the breaker panel is often in the basement, a utility room, the garage or in a box outside the house. The main breaker is the largest switch, usually at the top.
**When to shut it off:** if there is damaged wiring, sparking, a burning smell from the wiring, wet appliances or an electrical fire (see the electrical fire guide in this pack).
**How:**
1. Turn off the individual circuit breakers first.
2. Then turn off the main breaker.
3. To restore power, reverse the order: main breaker first, then each circuit, one at a time.
**Never** touch the panel if you are wet, standing in water or unsure it is safe: leave and ask for help. And do not touch switches if you suspect a gas leak.

## Gas
**Where it usually is:** the main shut-off valve is next to the meter, the first valve on the pipe coming out of the ground, usually outside the house.
**When to shut it off, and only then:** if you smell gas, hear it escaping or suspect a leak, or if officials tell you to. Do not turn it off "just in case".
**How:**
1. With a wrench, turn the lever until it sits across the pipe.
2. Leave it off. **Never turn it back on yourself**: only a qualified professional can restore gas, and it can take a long time; without gas there is no gas heating or cooking.
If the valve will not turn easily, do not force it: get out and call the gas company.

## The general rule
Turn a utility off only if you suspect the lines are damaged, you suspect a leak or officials instruct you to, and only if you can do it safely. When in doubt about gas, or about electricity and water together, the right decision is to leave and call the professionals.
