The poisoning guide in NIDO Essentials explains what carbon monoxide (CO) does to the body. This guide is about the devices that warn you: they are different, they go in specific places, and they only save lives if they are tested. An alarm with a dead battery is the same as no alarm at all.

## They are not the same device
- A **smoke alarm** warns of fire. Smoke rises: it goes on the ceiling or high on a wall.
- A **CO alarm** warns of a gas you can neither see nor smell, produced when furnaces, heaters, water heaters, fireplaces and generators burn fuel badly. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for its height and placement.
- **Combination** alarms that detect both smoke and CO exist. A CO alarm does not detect smoke, and a smoke alarm does not detect CO.

## Where to put them, and how many
**Smoke alarms:**
- Inside **every bedroom**.
- **Outside each sleeping area** (for example, in the hallway).
- On **every level** of the home, including the basement.
- Best **interconnected**: when one sounds, they all sound.
- Away from the kitchen to reduce false alarms: at least **10 feet (3 metres)** from the stove.
Example: a two-storey house with three bedrooms together upstairs and a basement needs 3 in the bedrooms + 1 in the upstairs hallway + 1 on the ground floor + 1 in the basement = **6 smoke alarms**.
**CO alarms:**
- In a central location **outside each sleeping area**.
- On **every level** of the home.
- Especially if you have fuel-burning appliances (gas, wood, coal, kerosene), a fireplace or an attached garage.
People who are deaf or hard of hearing can use special alarms with strobe lights and bed shakers.

## Testing and batteries
1. **Test every alarm once a month** using its test button, until it sounds loudly. If alarms are interconnected, check that they all sound.
2. **Replaceable batteries: change them at least once a year.** If an alarm chirps to warn of a low battery, change it right away; never leave an alarm without a battery because it "annoys you".
3. **Sealed 10-year batteries:** these are never changed; when the unit signals end of life, replace the whole unit.
4. Gently clear dust and cobwebs, following the manufacturer's instructions: dust can block the sensor.
5. After changing a battery, test the alarm again.

## When to replace the whole unit
- Smoke alarms are replaced **10 years** after their manufacture date (printed on the back): the sensor ages even if the test button still sounds.
- CO alarms are replaced on the date the manufacturer gives (depending on the model, between 5 and 10 years).
- If an alarm does not respond properly to the test, replace it sooner.

## If an alarm sounds
**If the smoke alarm sounds:**
1. Get everyone out of the house, following your escape plan.
2. Once outside, call the emergency number. Do not go back in.
**If the CO alarm sounds:**
1. Move to fresh air right away, with all people and pets.
2. Make sure everyone is accounted for.
3. Call the emergency number or the gas company from outside. Do not go back in until professionals say it is safe.

## Important
