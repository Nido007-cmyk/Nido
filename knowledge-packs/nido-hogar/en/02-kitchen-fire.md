Cooking is the most common cause of home fires, and a grease fire is the trickiest: you put it out by covering the pan, never with water. This guide covers a fire that has just started; if it grows, the answer is to get out (see the house fire guide in NIDO Essentials).

## Before it happens
- Stay in the kitchen while you fry, grill, broil or boil. If you leave, turn the burner off.
- Always keep a lid or a baking sheet nearby, next to the stove.
- Turn pan handles toward the back, and keep towels, paper and packaging away from the burners.
- Keep children and pets at least 3 feet (about a metre) from a lit stove.
- If oil starts to smoke, it is too hot: turn the heat off and let it cool.

## If the oil in a pan catches fire
1. Alert everyone else and keep your way out clear.
2. **Never throw water on it**: water splatters the burning oil and spreads the fire. Do not move the pan either: you can spill burning oil on yourself or across the stove.
3. Wearing an oven mitt, **slide the lid** (or a flat baking sheet) over the pan from the side, so it covers it completely. Do not drop it from above.
4. **Turn off the burner.**
5. **Leave the lid on** and do not touch the pan until it has completely cooled: if you uncover it too soon, the fire can start again.

## Fire in the oven or microwave
1. Turn the appliance off.
2. **Keep the door closed.** With no fresh air, the fire stays inside and goes out.
3. Do not open the door until the fire is completely out.
4. If flames escape the oven, try nothing more: leave the house and call the emergency number from outside.

## When to give up and get out
Leave immediately, closing the kitchen door behind you if you can, if any of these happen:
- The fire leaves the pan or reaches the hood, cabinets, curtains or ceiling.
- You cannot slide the lid on without reaching through the flames.
- There is so much smoke that you cannot see well or breathing is hard.
- You tried once and the fire is still going.
Once outside, call your emergency number and do not go back in.
